Proposal · 2026-06-16

Pro Mode

A megamenu of piano voicings — the canonical vocabulary the greats reached for, that doesn't fit on six strings.
15 voicings · 6 schools · ~3 hours implementation

The current voicing menu covers guitar-playable families. Drop-2 / drop-3 / shells / triads / sus / add9 / quartal / strum — every one of them is a grip a human hand can take on six strings.

The piano vocabulary that defines modern jazz comping doesn't translate. Bill Evans's rootless A voicings, McCoy Tyner's spread 4ths, Mehldau's 10ths, the upper-structure triads from Coltrane sessions — none of these are reachable on guitar because the intervals are wider than five frets, or the note count exceeds six strings, or both hands are needed independently.

Pro mode opens that vocabulary only when the instrument is piano, and only when explicitly enabled. The basic menu stays clean — no redundancy, no overwhelm for the user who's playing guitar or learning the foundations. AI play can then pull from this expanded pool when the user opts in.

Tier 01 · Bebop Foundations

Rootless vocabulary

The most-played voicings in jazz piano history. Two-handed comping with bass note implied (upright bass plays it). Four notes close-position around middle C — the dense bunched-cluster sound that defines bebop piano.
Rootless A — m7 / m9
Bill Evans · Wynton Kelly · Red Garland · Hank Jones
3 — 5 — b7 — 9

Dm7 voiced as F-A-C-E. Four notes close-position above middle C; the D root is implied (upright bass plays it). Pattern is movable: the 3rd of the chord goes in the bottom of the voicing, then stack 5-b7-9 above it.

Why piano-only: the dense 4-note cluster around middle C is THE bebop sound. Guitar drop-2 covers the same notes but spread across non-adjacent strings — the bunched-cluster character is lost. Needs an upright bass in the trio context.

Hear it: Kind of Blue — Wynton Kelly LH on "Freddie Freeloader"
Rootless B — m7 / m9
Bill Evans · Wynton Kelly · Red Garland
b7 — 9 — 3 — 5

Dm7 voiced as C-E-F-A. Same four notes as the A voicing but rotated — the b7 sits at the bottom now. A and B alternate every other ii-V-I so the voice-leading stays smooth (m7 A → V7 B → Imaj7 A → ii B …).

Why piano-only: the F-A interval at the top is a major 3rd inside the voicing — that's the bebop signature. Guitar can play it but the rotation breaks the smooth A↔B chain that's the point.

Hear it: Sunday at the Village Vanguard — Bill Evans entire ii-V-I vocabulary
Rootless A — dom7 / 13
Bill Evans · Hank Jones · Cedar Walton
b7 — 9 — 3 — 13

G7 voiced as F-A-B-E. The dom7 partner to the m7 A voicing. The 13th (E) sits on top, the b7 (F) on bottom — that's the natural tension of a dominant ready to resolve.

Why piano-only: the b9 / #11 / b13 alterations slot in by moving ONE note a half-step. Guitar voicings can't pivot that smoothly between altered and unaltered. The voicing IS designed to morph.

Hear it: any standard ii-V-I from the Bill Evans Trio canon
Powell Shell
Bud Powell · Wynton Kelly · Phineas Newborn
R — 3 — b7

G7 voiced as G-B-F in the LH only. Three-note grip leaving the RH free to blow eighth-note lines over the top. The single most economical voicing in bebop comping — minimum notes, maximum harmonic clarity.

Why piano-friendly: the LH stays in one position while the RH solos. Same economy works on guitar shell voicings (we have them), but the piano version sits BELOW middle C — the deeper register supports the soloist better.

Hear it: Bud Powell — The Amazing Bud Powell Vol. 1
Tier 02 · Modal & Spread

Stacked fourths

When the music got modal in 1959, bebop voicings became too "vertical." McCoy Tyner, Bill Evans, and Herbie Hancock built voicings out of 4ths instead of 3rds. Wider, more ambiguous, more open.
So What — full 5-note
Bill Evans · Miles Davis (as composer)
5 — R — 11 — b7 — b3

The literal "So What" chord for Dm: E-A-D-G-C from bass up — four stacked perfect 4ths. Five notes across one octave, played with both hands. The thumb crosses between LH and RH on this voicing.

Why piano-only: guitar quartal (we have) is the top 4 notes of this. The 5-note full version needs a hand span of an octave — possible on piano in a single grip, impossible on guitar in one shape.

Hear it: Kind of Blue — Bill Evans intro to "So What"
McCoy Tyner Spread
McCoy Tyner · Mulgrew Miller · Geri Allen
P5 bass · stacked 4ths in two registers

For Cm: C2 + G2 in LH (open 5th bass), then 4ths stacked from Eb3 up through Eb5. Eight notes spanning four octaves. The signature LH "bombs" McCoy drops under Coltrane's sheets-of-sound.

Why piano-only: 4-octave spread requires two-hand independence. The LH 5th sustains via pedal while the RH adds the upper structure. Guitar maxes out at 2-octave span in a single grip.

Hear it: McCoy Tyner on "Impressions" — A Love Supreme
Mehldau Tenth Spread
Brad Mehldau · Keith Jarrett · Bill Evans (late period)
R — 10 — 7 — 9 — 5

Cmaj7 voiced with C2 bass + E3 (10th up, a major 3rd one octave) + B3-D4-G4 upper structure. Three-handed sound — bass, middle voice, upper chord all distinct.

Why piano-only: the 10th interval (bass-to-3) is 16 semitones. Guitar drop-3 spread is 11 semitones max. The 10th is the iconic LH piano voicing — Earl Hines through Brad Mehldau used it constantly. Cannot be voiced on guitar without losing the open spread.

Hear it: Brad Mehldau — The Art of the Trio (any track)
Tier 03 · Upper Structures

Polychord tensions

A LH shell (3 + b7) plus a RH triad that's NOT the chord's root triad. The clash between the two creates altered, lydian, or polytonal colors instantly. The most flexible advanced vocabulary in jazz.
UST — V7#11 (Lydian Dominant)
Bill Evans · Cedar Walton · Mulgrew Miller
LH: R-3-b7 · RH: triad a whole step up

G7 voiced with G2-B2-F3 in LH + D4-F#4-A4 (D major triad) on top. The D major triad sits a major 2nd above G7's root — supplies the 9, #11, and 13 all at once. The "Joe Henderson" sound.

Why piano-only: the LH shell and RH triad occupy different registers — needs two-hand independence. The 6-note total exceeds guitar's 6-string capacity AND the registers don't sit on one fretboard span.

Hear it: Page One — Joe Henderson with McCoy Tyner
UST — V7alt (b9 b13)
Coltrane sessions · Chick Corea · Herbie Hancock
LH: R-b7-3 · RH: tritone-sub triad

G7 voiced with G2-F3-B3 LH + Db4-F4-Ab4 (Db major triad) on top. Db major contains the b9, #9, and b13 of G7 — the full "altered" sound from one triad. Resolves dramatically to Cmaj7 via the smooth tritone-sub voice-leading.

Why piano-only: the polychord stack (V7 + bV major triad = tritone-sub upper structure) is THE textbook altered voicing. Six distinct pitches required; the Db triad on top must be heard as a discrete unit, not blurred into the dominant — a thing only piano really delivers.

Hear it: Herbie Hancock on Miles Davis — Nefertiti
Locked Hands — Block chord
George Shearing · Red Garland · Errol Garner
Melody doubled in octave · 4-note close voicing between

Melody C5 on top, C4 in LH octave below, between them a close-position 4-note chord (E4-G4-B4). The whole grip moves in parallel as the melody moves — five notes locked together like one giant chord.

Why piano-only: parallel motion across 5 voices in close position over a sustained octave — needs full keyboard view. Iconic Shearing Quintet sound, every Garland recording. Guitar block chords exist but are 3 strings max, not 5 voices locked.

Hear it: George Shearing — Lullaby of Birdland
Tier 04 · Stride & Solo Piano

Two-handed independence

When there's no bass player. The LH alternates between a bass note and a chord stab; the RH plays melody. The bouncing pattern is the entire rhythm section condensed into one instrument.
LH 10th — Stride bass
Art Tatum · Oscar Peterson · Erroll Garner · James P. Johnson
Bass on 1 · 10th (R + 3) on 2 · chord stab on 2 & 4

Cmaj7 with C2 bass on beat 1, then E3 (the 10th = the 3rd up an octave) on beat 2 with G3-B3-D4 chord stab. The bouncing LH covers a 2-octave span between bass and stab.

Why piano-only: 10ths are the LH piano signature. Guitar can't reach 10ths (16 semitones = ~10 frets). The bouncing stride pattern (low note → mid chord → low note → mid chord) is impossible to sustain on a string instrument.

Hear it: Art Tatum — The Tatum Group Masterpieces
Phineas Octaves
Phineas Newborn · Oscar Peterson · André Previn
RH plays melody doubled an octave higher · LH covers chord

RH bebop line doubled in octaves (thumb + pinky), LH plays shell or rootless underneath. The doubling lights up the line with weight and clarity, used for the climaxes of bop choruses.

Why piano-only: two notes per RH thumb/pinky, played in parallel at high tempo — the RH octave technique. Guitar has octave lines (Wes Montgomery) but Wes used thumb-strikes; piano octaves are wrist-driven and faster.

Hear it: Phineas Newborn — The Great Jazz Piano of Phineas Newborn Jr.
Tier 05 · Neo-Soul & Gospel

Modern extensions

Black-American church and modern R&B harmony. Voicings stacked with 11s and 13s, half-diminished re-purposed as bright tonic, quartal stacks borrowed from McCoy and re-rhythmized for gospel feel.
Gospel Quartal Stack
Cory Henry · Bob James · Joe Sample · Donny Hathaway
Bass + LH 4ths + RH 4ths stacked

Cm with C2 bass, then F3-Bb3-Eb4 LH 4ths, then Eb4-Ab4-Db5 RH 4ths. Seven notes spread across three octaves. The "modern gospel" sound — open, prayerful, never bluesy in the Delta sense.

Why piano-only: 7+ notes in three distinct registers requires both hands plus pedal sustain. Guitar can suggest the sound with quartal (we have) but the layered registers — bass / mid / top — need three independent voices.

Hear it: Cory Henry on Snarky Puppy — "Lingus" solo
Glasper Half-Dim as I
Robert Glasper · D'Angelo · Brad Mehldau · José James session players
m7b5 voiced as bright maj9 over its b3 bass

Cm7b5 (C-Eb-Gb-Bb) re-voiced as Cm7b5/Eb with the 9 and 11 added — comes out sounding like an Eb maj9 with a sad C in the bass. The "Glasper sound" that makes m7b5 feel like a tonic instead of a tension chord.

Why piano-only: the re-spelling requires the LH bass to sustain through the RH's overtones — same fingering, totally different harmonic gravity. Piano pedal sustain is what sells the illusion.

Hear it: Robert Glasper — Black Radio intro
Tier 06 · Avant-garde

Color over convention

Voicings that step outside the functional-harmony grid. Monk's deliberate clashes, polychords from Stravinsky's piano works, dense clusters that work as approach colors or static drones.
Monk Cluster
Thelonious Monk · Andrew Hill · Don Pullen
m2 + m2 + M2 + m2 cluster — Monk's "wrong note" approach

G3-Ab3-A3-B3-C4 cluster — five notes inside a major 3rd. Used as an "approach voicing" (slam this, resolve to Cmaj7) or a static color block over a vamp. The half-step density is the Monk signature.

Why piano-only: the cluster needs the entire palm of one hand pressed flat on five adjacent notes. Guitar can't even produce minor 2nds on adjacent strings unless heavily detuned. The cluster IS the piano-only effect.

Hear it: Monk — Brilliant Corners
Polychord — D over C
Igor Stravinsky · Bill Evans · Lyle Mays · Egberto Gismonti
Bass note + entirely different triad above

C2 bass + D-F#-A (D major triad) in the RH. Reads as Cmaj7#11 or as "two keys at once" depending on context. Stravinsky used it in Petrushka; Bill Evans hid it inside lush extensions decades later.

Why piano-only: the bass note and the upper triad need to be heard as separate harmonic objects. Pedal sustains the bass while the RH triad cycles. On guitar the same notes blur together — no register separation.

Hear it: Stravinsky Petrushka · Bill Evans Peace Piece

UX details: the megamenu is gated behind a header-bar toggle that's visible only when the instrument is set to piano. Toggling it on opens the megamenu; toggling it off collapses everything back to the basic 9-family vocabulary. Selected voicings are remembered per-session in the same multi-select voicingPrefSet. AI play draws from the union of basic + selected Pro voicings, filtered by the chord's quality (each voicing declares which qualities it accepts).

Implementation roadmap

What's the lift. Honest sketch — not a final design.

1. New shape representation

  • Current shape is a 6-position fret array for guitar. Piano voicings can have 5–10 notes spanning four octaves; the fret format doesn't fit.
  • Add a pianoShape field on the voicing candidate: array of MIDI numbers, e.g. [36, 52, 59, 62, 67] for the Mehldau 10th.
  • When state.instrument === 'piano' AND the family is in the Pro set, render from pianoShape. Otherwise use the existing 6-string shape.

2. New builders

  • One builder per Pro voicing: rootlessAVoicing(quality, root), mccoySpreadVoicing(quality, root), etc.
  • Each builder returns a MIDI-array shape, gated by quality (e.g. USTV7Sharp11_OK = new Set(['7'])).
  • Add them as new branches in _pickShape dispatcher.

3. New VOICING_OPTIONS entries

  • Add 15 new family ids: rootlessA, rootlessB, shellLH, soWhatFull, mccoySpread, mehldau10th, ustSharp11, ustAlt, lockedHands, stride10, phineasOct, gospelQuartal, glasperHalfDim, monkCluster, polychord.
  • Tag each with pro: true so they only surface in Pro mode.
  • Add SVG icons for each (same style as the existing 9).

4. PROGRESSION_VOICING weights

  • Pro voicings get progression-specific weights only on the relevant idioms: rootlessA / rootlessB on every bebop ii-V-I, mccoySpread on modal vamps, stride10 on solo-piano arrangements, etc.
  • Default weight on non-matching progressions is 0 — Pro voicings never sneak into a Bach chorale.

5. UI gating

  • "Pro Mode" toggle in the header, visible only when instrument is piano.
  • The megamenu opens beneath the toolbar as a full-width panel.
  • Selections persist in voicingPrefSet; the existing multi-select infrastructure handles the rest.

6. Order of work

  • Phase 1 (6 voicings): Rootless A m7 + dom7, Rootless B m7, Powell Shell, So What full, Mehldau 10th. The most-played, most-impactful. ~3 hours.
  • Phase 2 (4 voicings): UST #11, UST alt, Locked Hands, McCoy spread. Adds the upper-structure vocabulary. ~3 hours.
  • Phase 3 (5 voicings): Stride, Octaves, Gospel quartal, Glasper, Monk cluster, Polychord. Round out the schools. ~4 hours.
