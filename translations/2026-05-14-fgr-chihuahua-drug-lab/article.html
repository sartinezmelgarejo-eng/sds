This article by Fernanda Monroy originally appeared in the May 14, 2026 edition of Contralínea, Mexico's leading investigative journalism magazine.

With the aim of determining whether the Chihuahua State Attorney General's Office overstepped federal jurisdiction during the operation carried out at an alleged drug laboratory located in the municipality of Morelos, Chihuahua — in which agents of the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) participated — the Federal Attorney General's Office (FGR) opened a new line of investigation and reported irregularities in evidence handling and the chain of custody of the case.

The FGR announced, through an official statement, that the federal investigation was initiated after state authorities notified them by telephone of the property's seizure on April 18, "after the operation had already been planned and executed."

According to the federal agency, upon arriving at the site, ministerial agents and forensic experts from the Criminal Investigation Agency, with federal forces support, did not receive the secured area nor the evidence and substances found from local authorities under proper chain of custody protocols.

The attorney general's office noted that these irregularities were documented in the federal case file through an official report, which recorded anomalies related to the safeguarding of the property and the handling of evidence.

In response, the FGR opened a new line of investigation to determine whether during the operation carried out on April 17 and 18, state authorities "overstepped federal jurisdiction" and whether they continued "violating the constitutional design of federal powers."

The institution emphasized that crimes related to clandestine laboratories, synthetic drug production, and possible links to organized crime fall under exclusive federal jurisdiction, as established by the General Health Law.

Likewise, the agency recalled that functions related to national security and foreign policy correspond solely to the federal government, meaning state entities are prohibited from establishing alliances or agreements with foreign governments.

The FGR also reported that the alleged drug laboratory remains under federal custody and has not yet been dismantled, as the process of destruction and transfer of substances and objects found continues under security protocols.

"The most notable aspect in this regard is to reiterate that the FGR is working to clarify the facts and identify those responsible," the institution stated.

In parallel, the Special Prosecutor's Office for the Investigation and Litigation of Complex Cases maintains an open case file for possible crimes related to national security. According to the FGR, most of the individuals summoned have already given their ministerial interviews; however, seven people did not appear and will be summoned again, along with other state public servants considered relevant to the lines of investigation.
