This article by Arturo Sánchez Jiménez and Alma E. Muñoz originally appeared in the May 14, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. With a maximum speed of 50 kilometers per hour, the capacity to charge in a household outlet, and a design tailored for the narrow streets of cities and towns, the federal government yesterday showed the first images of the prototype of the Olinia, a Mexican electric minivehicle that will be officially presented on June 7 and will begin production in 2027.

At the morning press conference at the National Palace, President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo also reported that in July the cargo version will be presented, and that starting in August or September construction will begin on the infrastructure needed to manufacture the vehicle. The first plant will have an initial capacity to produce 20,000 units per year, with the goal of reaching 50,000 within four years.

"The prototype is ready," the president affirmed, noting that Olinia is part of Plan México and seeks to create a national automotive brand. "Mexico only assembles vehicles. So the objective is to have a brand of our own," she pointed out.

The Secretary of Science, Humanities, Technology and Innovation, Rosaura Ruiz Gutiérrez, stated that the project seeks to bring electromobility "closer to Mexican homes through an accessible, safe and efficient vehicle."

The project coordinator, Roberto Capuano Tripp, explained that the vehicle was conceived to respond to real urban mobility needs. He added that the vehicle is "wide and spacious, comfortable," and that its speed is designed for urban journeys.

Capuano indicated that the model also has export possibilities to Latin American countries. "Our most interesting export market is not to the north, but to the south," he noted, mentioning that various governments in the region have expressed interest in Olinia.

He further explained that the batteries were designed by the Olinia team itself to integrate into circular economy schemes. After completing their useful life in the vehicle, he explained, they can be reused for energy storage in homes and later recycled in Sonora.
