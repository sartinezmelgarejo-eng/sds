This article by Nancy Flores originally appeared in the May 16, 2026 edition of Revista Contralínea, Mexico's leading investigative journalism magazine.

One of the most important challenges in Mexican politics is preventing organized crime, particularly drug trafficking, from infiltrating the branches of government and all three levels of government through candidacies or political party financing. In this regard, the new president of Morena explains to Contralínea that the ruling party is strengthening its mechanisms to shut the door on criminal elements, with the cooperation of the Security Cabinet, the Financial Intelligence Unit (UIF), and INE oversight. Ariadna Montiel recalls that the foundational struggle of the National Regeneration Movement, with AMLO at the helm, was to combat corruption and the privileges enjoyed at public expense, and that the principles of the political institute she now leads are precisely "don't lie, don't steal, and don't betray the people," which will govern her leadership. Another challenge the former Secretary of Welfare will emphasize concerns the tensions between Mexico and the United States; for this reason, she announces a steadfast defense of the homeland and sovereignty, beginning with the march on Saturday, May 16, in Chihuahua. In the interview, she also dismisses as unfounded the criticisms against her alleging she would take the welfare program registries to Morena: that is a serious crime punishable by prison, she reminds.

The federal Security Cabinet — which includes civilian and military intelligence agencies — the Financial Intelligence Unit, and the oversight bodies of the National Electoral Institute (INE) will be strategic in strengthening the mechanisms available to the National Regeneration Movement Party (Morena) to prevent the infiltration of organized crime, particularly drug trafficking, into candidacies and campaign financing, assures the new president of the political institute, Ariadna Montiel Reyes.

Interviewed by Contralínea at the ruling party's offices, the leader emphasizes that the screening process for aspiring candidates for elected office will be exhaustive. "All candidates, all, all, all of them, will undergo analysis by security agencies. Both we as Morena [will conduct a rigorous examination], and I understand the electoral authority must also proceed accordingly to detect, where applicable, any situation we might not catch from the evaluation we carry out."

Montiel Reyes states that "combating organized crime is a task for the country's security forces, for the Security Cabinet as a whole, and we will ask for their support to run [candidates] through the filter of government intelligence review, so that no one is linked to criminal activity, of any kind."

Due to this type of vulnerability, two recent cases have put Morena in the spotlight: the U.S. Department of Justice's accusation against the governor on leave from Sinaloa, Rubén Rocha Moya, and nine other current or former officials from that state, alleging ties to the Chapitos faction of the Pacific Cartel; and the case of Hernán Bermúdez Requena, former Secretary of Public Security of Tabasco — appointed to that position by Senator Adán Augusto López Hernández when he was governor — as Bermúdez Requena allegedly led La Barredora, a criminal group involved in extortion and fuel trafficking in the state.

And although in the first case, the Mexican government has so far insisted that the United States has not delivered evidence, it is known that the Department of Justice has testimony from the Chapitos themselves — brothers Ovidio and Joaquín Guzmán López, sons of El Chapo Guzmán, who are seeking to become protected witnesses for the U.S. government in exchange for procedural benefits — in addition to whatever information Gerardo Mérida Sánchez may provide. Mérida Sánchez, the former Secretary of Public Security of Sinaloa under Rocha Moya's government, voluntarily surrendered to U.S. authorities on May 15 seeking a deal, and was detained and transferred to New York for prosecution.

Nevertheless, beyond these two cases, the challenge of preventing organized crime, and particularly drug trafficking, from infiltrating the branches of government and all three levels of government belongs to all of Mexican politics, not just the ruling party. An example of this is what occurred under governments of other parties: under the PAN's Felipe Calderón, the federal Secretary of Public Security (from December 2006 to November 2012), Genaro García Luna, allied himself with the Sinaloa Cartel while supposedly leading the fight against drug trafficking. He was sentenced in October 2024 in New York to 38 years in prison.

Other similar cases include: PAN member Francisco Javier García Cabeza de Vaca, former governor of Tamaulipas (2016-2022), who faces federal charges for organized crime, money laundering, and tax fraud; as well as PRI members: Mario Villanueva Madrid, former governor of Quintana Roo (1993-1999), sentenced in both Mexico and the United States for collaborating with the Juárez Cartel; Tomás Yarrington, former governor of Tamaulipas (1999-2004), sentenced in the United States to 9 years in prison for accepting bribes from the Gulf Cartel; Eugenio Hernández, former governor of Tamaulipas (2005-2010), accused of money laundering and ties to organized crime; Roberto Sandoval, former governor of Nayarit (2011-2017), sanctioned by the U.S. Treasury Department and prosecuted in Mexico for illicit financial operations; and Édgar Veytia, former attorney general of Nayarit, sentenced in a U.S. court to 20 years in prison.

To this must be added that, during the PRI era in government, Miguel Nazar Haro — who was director of the political police, the Federal Security Directorate (DFS) — was implicated in protecting the founders of the now-defunct Guadalajara Cartel and in crimes linked to the Dirty War period. The DFS operated between 1947 and 1985, and in the 1970s-1980s was considered a "bridge" between the government and organized crime. In fact, the leaders of the Guadalajara Cartel — Miguel Ángel Félix Gallardo (the Jefe de Jefes), Rafael Caro Quintero, Ernesto Fonseca Carrillo (Don Neto), and Amado Carrillo Fuentes (el Señor de los Cielos) — operated as "agents" of that institution and carried the infamous charolas [credentials] of the DFS.

Don't lie, don't steal, and don't betray the people

Asked about the fight against corruption led by President López Obrador and the campaigns branding Morena and its representatives in power as narco-president, narco-candidate, narco-party, and narco-government — driven by the political and media right, now taking advantage of the cases the U.S. justice system is building against current and former Sinaloa officials — Ariadna Montiel Reyes considers that the party's principles are those of not lying, not stealing, and not betraying the people.

"The first thing we must be very clear about is that the fight against corruption was the fundamental axis of our movement's struggle throughout all the years that López Obrador led it. And that corruption was what had impoverished our country. And the privileges ended, the lavish spending ended, the bonuses in the federal government ended since President López Obrador took office.

"Having worked with both, with President [Claudia Sheinbaum] an important austerity policy has also been implemented. From the day we arrived at Sedesol to the day I handed over the Ministry of Welfare, we reduced half of the bureaucratic structure that operates the Ministry and prioritized fieldwork and closeness with the people. And the president contributed an important part, but the president [Sheinbaum] perhaps doesn't talk about it as much, but she is also carrying out an important austerity exercise, a more efficient budget operation. And there has been tremendous progress.

"So, the Fourth Transformation has achieved that. And in this year when welfare programs have a social investment of 1 trillion pesos, we ask: 'and where was that trillion pesos before?' In the pockets of a few, in the businesses of a few. That trillion that exists today, where was that trillion in the past? So, we must vindicate the fight against corruption not as a matter of administrative procedure, but as an ethical principle of our Movement. And we are going to do the work of raising awareness among everyone that we must continue on this path, that this is the greatest force that has allowed us to help people and expand our programs. And we must start with ourselves."

Montiel Reyes adds that, at its core, Morena has those three principles of not stealing, not lying, and not betraying the people of Mexico, and therefore the party she now leads must always walk that path. "And we have clearly stated that governments born from Morena must work with honesty and that those who represent us must also be honest people; that first and foremost, that is an ethical-political condition. But, furthermore, we must also say that the narrative the opposition is building has to do with the fact that this is our greatest strength, and that is where we have been able to consolidate the Fourth Transformation. And so they want to attack us where it matters most to us."

In that context, the president of Morena criticizes the leaders of the opposition parties. "Alito Moreno [Alejandro Moreno, PRI leader] comes to question us. And it's not me saying it, it's the people and the polls: the PRI is the most rejected political party in the country, and its current national leader is the worst-rated figure today. So, we also need to put into proper perspective who is saying it; and we must maintain our body and method of informing the people to tell them the truth."

Regarding the political right and the National Action Party, Montiel Reyes observes that they have formed an alliance with some media outlets to maintain a permanent attack on the National Regeneration Movement, but warns that the people don't believe them, because they receive the support and development projects that they didn't get before. "So, people say: 'well, I have my resources, nobody has stolen anything from me, on the contrary, the Fourth Transformation project is supporting me, is considering me. And there are more and more actions benefiting the people.' So, that is countered with good actions from the governments, from the president's government, from our fellow governors."

The national leader also recognizes the work of the Movement's legislators, as they have approved the constitutional reforms benefiting the people, and notes that the party's commitment is to create the conditions for the path to remain one of honesty, which is why they will also seek to recover the concept of courageous honesty.

Montiel Reyes observes that the Movement's principles have made it possible to reduce poverty in the country, as that is achieved only through investment and budget. "But we must be very courageous in going out into the streets, informing, raising awareness, and not backing down; and as I also said the day I was elected president of Morena: to hold our heads high because we have maintained loyalty to the project. And whoever doesn't feel they belong here, let them know too: here in Morena things are done this way and we will walk this path. It is the path that legitimately brought us to power, and we want to be in power not for power itself, but to continue supporting the people and building welfare for the people."

Will there be more filters to prevent corruption? she is asked.

"Well, the survey filter is very important. It's not a survey that simply measures whether people know you or not. The survey measures recognition, but it also asks about your attributes, your honesty, your closeness to the people, your knowledge of the territory, which implies knowing the people's problems and also whether people are willing for you to represent them. So, these are much broader values than what we typically see published, about whether this or that person is more famous. Because otherwise we could just put up many famous people, and that's not what it's about: politics is something else. And the filters have to do with evaluating honesty and all these attributes and principles that the people see in each of the candidates."

So, when the time comes, these are part of the elements that will be taken into account for deciding candidacies, and everyone must walk — as my mother says — along the straight line we were taught since childhood. And if someone along the way can get confused, but that's where we also need to be. And the colleagues who come to join from other political forces or other political backgrounds must be valued and accepted. Morena must keep its doors open, but we also have the responsibility to guide that building process.

"They asked me: 'And accept people who come from outside and so on?' Well, we live in the work of convincing others of our Movement. So, we cannot close Morena's doors, but we must be a guide for the Movement."

That is why I am so convinced and will put my greatest efforts into territorial work, which is what I have done my whole life and know how to do, because I believe that is also where we face reality, it makes us aware too.

Transparency and accountability in the party

Historically, political parties have not lived up to transparency and accountability standards. What will your plan be in that regard? Because ultimately there is a general budget for parties exceeding 7 billion pesos and Morena has the largest budget, she is asked.

"Yes, this is very important. Morena has contributed to transparency in the use of resources, which is not just transparency for transparency's sake, but there is also republican austerity here, and the resources allocated to the political party should be applied fundamentally to the territory, to fieldwork. And well, to comply with all electoral regulations which are increasingly complex, especially regarding fiscal oversight. And that's what it's about: ensuring that the resources used by political parties are of institutional origin through INE or whatever the law allows. And to that extent, also being transparent."

"It's about ensuring that all the information that should be on the portals is there, and complying with data protection law, which is also very important — the registries and members. All the members who currently participate, we will in the coming days establish a full review of this part, which is very important because these are significant sums and we have always been convinced that these resources should be reduced."

"That didn't happen in the electoral reform and hopefully it will by 2027. Today we will move toward Plan C, which will give us the strength to continue advancing the democratization of the country and make democracy cost the country less and less, making it more of a culture than an administrative activity, as it has largely been until now. And we must maintain this transparency in the use of resources because they also belong to the people, they don't belong to the parties, they are for a task, and they must be managed with full transparency."

"Furthermore, in the party, resources are for outreach, for paying our colleagues in the field, for the actual operation of the National Political Institute and in the states. So, having come from government with all its regulations and restrictions, I say, 'oh well, here it's for political activity.' That is transparent and we are going to invest a lot in the newspaper Regeneración."

Defense of the homeland and sovereignty

Asked about the tensions Mexico faces due to actions by the U.S. government, and the need and urgency to defend sovereignty, to defend our homeland, Montiel Reyes states that this will be part of her action as president of Morena:

"I think this is the most important issue at this juncture. It has to do not only with our Movement, which is very important, it has to do with the entire nation. It has to do with the depths of who we are as Mexicans. And we have lived through this situation in which the possibility of foreign interference has been growing, but the gravest part of this is that it has been promoted by Mexicans, especially by the opposition, by the right, which calls for intervention under a pretext that seems to me to be just that — a pretext — that of 'security,' which while certainly an important issue, so important that the president has devoted her efforts throughout her government, the opposition is clearly seeking help from abroad."

"Now we are seeing the visit of the president of the Community of Madrid [Isabel Díaz Ayuso] with positions that disrespect our country, our sovereignty. So, what the opposition is doing is looking abroad for what it has not been able to build within. They have always been far from the people. In the neoliberal period they were able to sustain themselves because they did it by force, with electoral fraud, buying people's consciences, deceiving people. Today that is not the case because our movement has made a very important contribution in raising awareness and providing information. And today the people are informed, they know they have rights, they defend their rights."

"So, they are distant from the people and have no possibility of advancing electorally, and that is why they seek help from foreign countries. And this is a moment, a juncture, in which we as a Movement must give our full support to our president and to the homeland, because while we represent a national project, our fundamental objective is for welfare to reach the people. So, this is a supremely transcendental moment in which politics is going to be divided — or is already divided — between those who want a foreign government or foreign governments to intervene in our vision as a country, and the Mexican men and women who want to maintain our sovereignty and continue building ourselves as a nation."

It has always been said that Morena is a very young party and yet it is facing these difficult, tense, complex junctures. And there is also a kind of urgency and a complaint that the party has not been present during these year and a half of tensions that President Sheinbaum has had to face. How could the party actually mobilize, actually be present from a much more strategic, central position in the defense of the homeland, in the convocation of the people themselves?

"This is very important. In Morena this has also been something very important in this last year and a half because it is the militant organization that is also going to help us reproduce and strengthen the struggle. So, this year and a half before we took charge of representing the movement, this has been built and it is a fundamental base to continue the struggle. But, on the other hand, what we have at this moment is the defense of sovereignty as a central action of our political action. So, what are we going to do? We are going to mobilize, the way we know how. The fact that we came to power in the presidency of the Republic does not prevent us from continuing to mobilize to defend the rights of all."

"We are going to work, first, to continue raising awareness among the membership, among the movement's structures, among the people's representatives, that the battle is in the territory and that we must do it house by house. Also, the day I was sworn in, I told them that we are going to visit at least 30 million households to bring the newspaper Regeneración and to bring our project, our thinking, and call on the population to defend what we have all built together. These are achievements, triumphs, what the government of the advanced Fourth Transformation has accomplished for the people. And it is the people who must defend it and we must push for this through our activism, our work. Organization is transformation."

"This is what I learned throughout my life, that this is how we arrived with López Obrador at the presidency of the Republic, how we reaffirmed that our President Claudia Sheinbaum would reach the presidency of the Republic — it's right there. That is our terrain because it is also the ethically correct one, to be with the people, to be there."

"We are going to mobilize and we are going to inform and we are going to raise awareness and we are going to organize better, that is why the work our colleagues on the National Executive Committee have done to organize the movement is so important."

"So I also told them, 'this is not a bureaucratic task, this is a political task'; because organizing means being prepared, bringing information to the people, maintaining the bond we have with the people, and if we do this we will resist in the moments we are living through, but furthermore we will also strengthen ourselves. That is where our strength is, where our love for the people is."

Would there be mass mobilizations at some point?

"Well, to start we are watching the situation, but if necessary we will call on the people to organize — first, because although it may seem methodical, that is the way we have been able to consolidate the project over time. Morena as a political party has officially been registered since 2014, but we founded it in 2010 as a civil association and since then — well, since always, but since then in this Morena phase — we have done house-by-house visits, structured the committees. Now the municipal committees are being established and what we want is to give them an action plan."

"And also to strongly instill this feeling that the struggle does not end, that we continue in the struggle, and we see it now, and that we must be prepared, organizing, calling on the people, informing, and if necessary... Well, we are already seeing it in the state of Chihuahua, where the governor intervened through a U.S. agency on security matters and has refused to appear before the Chihuahua Congress. She didn't want to come to the Senate, she doesn't want to go to the Chihuahua Congress, where she is first and foremost required to report, and we consider that this position is not just a political debate — it affects the population of Mexico, especially that of Chihuahua."

"So, there is significant anger among the people and we will give direction so that our struggle, which is always peaceful, involves the people. That is fundamental, that the people make the demand their own. And we see the indignation over the visit of Ms. Ayuso. And we will always be stating our position."
