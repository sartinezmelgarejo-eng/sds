This article by Alonso Urrutia originally appeared in the May 17, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Mérida, Yucatán — During the inauguration of the new Agustín O'Horán Hospital in this city — one of the largest in Latin America, built with an investment of five billion pesos — President Claudia Sheinbaum declared it a symbol of the shift in health policy: the definitive abandonment of the concept of health as a commodity, replaced by its recognition as a fundamental social right. She affirmed that during her six-year term, 180 billion pesos will be invested in the construction and renovation of hospital infrastructure.

Sheinbaum said she is convinced that without corruption, public resources are sufficient: "We are living through a historic era for the health system. Since October 2024, 29 hospital projects have been built — seven from IMSS, 18 from IMSS-Bienestar, and four from ISSSTE."

"Nothing and no one will stop the transformation"

The president framed these health initiatives within the broader project of national transformation: "Nothing and no one will stop the transformation of our homeland, because this movement was born from the people, grew with the people, and governs for the people. The days of privilege, corruption, and plunder of our country are over."

Today Mexico walks with its head held high, with dignity, with hope, with justice, and no one, absolutely no one, will be able to stop the force of a conscious, organized people determined to defend their rights, their future, and their hope. Because when the people unite for justice, there is no power that can stop them.

The hospital inauguration became the stage for what amounted to an informal report on health sector advances and a defense of popular identity under the government of the transformation.

The numbers: a health system being rebuilt

Before an audience of dozens of IMSS-Bienestar doctors, Sheinbaum said that for decades public health was neglected — until this movement arrived in government. Where previously 6,500 medical specialists were trained per year, in 2026 the number will reach 14,000. Where IMSS-Bienestar hospitals provided 37.5 million consultations in 2023, projections for this year point to 60 million.

No more cash registers: healthcare will be free

During the ceremony — which also marked Yucatán's formal incorporation into the IMSS-Bienestar system, bringing the total to 24 states — IMSS-Bienestar director Alejandro Svarch announced that alongside the hospital's opening, the payment windows will be permanently shut: all services will be completely free of charge.

This is not a mere administrative act, because with it, families will no longer confuse the fear of illness with the fear of not having money to pay for medical care.
