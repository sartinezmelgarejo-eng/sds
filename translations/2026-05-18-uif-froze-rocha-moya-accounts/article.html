This article by Anahí Del Ángel originally appeared in the May 18, 2026 edition of Revista Contralínea, a Mexican investigative journalism outlet.

The Financial Intelligence Unit (UIF) froze the accounts of Sinaloa's governor on leave, Rubén Rocha Moya, as a preventive measure, since it is a mechanism that banks implement due to their financial relationship with foreign entities. This was prompted by the United States arrest warrant against ten Mexican individuals, President Claudia Sheinbaum explained.

The president clarified that "these measures do not mean that the UIF is conducting a direct investigation against those named," but rather that they are standardized procedures derived from agreements and information shared between banking authorities in the face of international legal situations.

Subsequently, the agency reported through an official statement that it had made additions to the Blocked Persons List (LPB) on a strictly preventive basis, as part of actions aimed at protecting the integrity of the national financial system.

According to the agency, which operates under the Ministry of Finance and Public Credit, the measure was adopted based on reports issued by institutions within the Mexican financial system, following public statements made by United States authorities.

The UIF explained that, because several Mexican banks maintain correspondent relationships with United States financial entities, internal compliance and monitoring mechanisms were activated, resulting in alerts regarding clients identified as Politically Exposed Persons. Based on these reports, known as 24-Hour Blocked Persons Lists, the financial authority proceeded with the preventive freezing of assets.

The agency emphasized that these actions "do not constitute a definitive resolution nor do they imply an attribution of criminal, administrative, or financial responsibility."

Likewise, it clarified that individuals included on the LPB retain all legal guarantees provided under Mexican law, including the right to a hearing, as well as the ability to file administrative and judicial appeals to contest the measure.
