This article by Clara Zepeda originally appeared in the May 26, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Mexico's exports and imports posted historically high figures in April, the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) revealed when releasing its merchandise trade balance report.

Exports reached 72.042 billion dollars, the highest level on record, with an annual increase of 32.6 percent compared to April 2025.

As for imports, these totaled 67.5218 billion dollars — also a record-breaking figure that reflects an annual advance of 24.1 percent, INEGI specified.

The United States remains a central market for Mexican merchandise. 83.6 percent of the 72.042 billion dollars Mexico exported in April were destined for that country.

A 32.6 percent increase in the value of exports had not been seen since May 2021. The performance was driven mainly by sales of non-oil merchandise, which grew 33.5 percent year-over-year.

Manufacturing exports advanced 34 percent, while non-automotive ones grew 45.8; automotive, 8.2; and extractive, 71 percent. Oil sales registered a rise of 7.9 percent.

In the January–April 2026 period, the value of total exports added up to 247.628 billion dollars, an amount that represented annual growth of 21.8 percent, due to a 23.4 percent increase in non-oil exports and a 17.2 percent decline in oil exports.

In the first four months of 2026, the structure by value of merchandise shipments was: manufactured goods, 91.1 percent; agricultural, 3.3 percent; non-oil extractive, 3 percent; and oil products, 2.6 percent.

“The performance of non-automotive manufacturing stands out, having offset various sources of weakness, while the automotive sector shows signs of slowdown in an environment of heightened uncertainty over the review of the trilateral T-MEC trade pact,” the analysis division of Valmex Casa de Bolsa stated.

Mexican Purchases Gain Momentum

In April 2026, the value of merchandise imports was 67.522 billion dollars — a record level, representing an annual rebound of 24.1 percent.

Purchases of foreign consumer goods reached 8.499 billion dollars, a figure that meant an annual rise of 7.7 percent. That rate resulted from an 11 percent advance in non-oil consumer goods imports and a 5.3 percent drop in oil ones (gasoline and butane and propane gas).

Intermediate-use goods worth 54.228 billion dollars were imported, 29.8 percent higher than reported in April of last year. That figure in turn derived from a 31.9 percent advance in non-oil intermediate-use imports and 3.8 percent in oil intermediate-use goods.

Imports of capital goods reached 4.794 billion dollars, implying an annual rise of 1.3 percent.

In the first four months of 2026, the accumulated value of total imports was 244.120 billion dollars, 19.9 percent more than observed in the same period of 2025. Non-oil purchases rose 21.5 percent and oil ones, only 1.1 annually.

Thus, Mexico registered a trade surplus of 4.520 billion dollars, which compares to the 5.932 billion dollars reported in March. For the first four months of 2026, the trade balance presented a surplus of 3.508 billion dollars. In the same period of 2025, it reported a deficit of 314 million dollars.

Valmex maintains that “the persistence of the surplus will depend on the capacity to deepen export dynamics outside the automotive segment, advance geographic diversification of trade flows, and strengthen national content within global value chains.”
