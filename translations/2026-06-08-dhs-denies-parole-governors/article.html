This article by La Redacción originally appeared in the June 8, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) denied that Alfonso Durazo, governor of Sonora, and Américo Villarreal, governor of Tamaulipas, have a "special permit" to enter and leave the country, as reported by the newspaper La Opinión, which published an information request it made to the agency in which the denial appears. The request was made after reports circulated that both governors had had their visas cancelled due to alleged investigations against them: "Francisco Alfonso Durazo Montaño and Américo Villareal Anaya have not been [granted such a permit]." The DHS permit, also known as Parole, is a discretionary authorization that allows foreigners to enter or remain temporarily in the United States, without a visa, on the premise that their stay generates a benefit to the government, serves public interest, or aids national security.
