This article by La Redacción originally appeared in the June 8, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) denied that Alfonso Durazo, governor of Sonora, and Américo Villarreal, governor of Tamaulipas, have a "special permit" to enter and leave the country, the newspaper La Opinión reported, after publishing an information request it had submitted to the agency in which the denial appears. La Opinión filed the request after reports circulated that both governors had had their visas cancelled over alleged investigations against them: "Francisco Alfonso Durazo Montaño and Américo Villareal Anaya have not been [granted such a permit]."

The DHS permit, also known as Parole, is a discretionary authorization that allows foreigners to enter or remain temporarily in the United States, without a visa, on the premise that their stay benefits the government, serves the public interest, or aids national security.
