This article by Cristina Gómez Lima originally appeared in the June 8, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Hermosillo, Sonora. A new Wastewater Treatment Plant (PTAR) began operations in the municipality of Puerto Peñasco, as a piece of infrastructure that seeks to address a backlog of more than five decades and to definitively close the oxidation lagoon used by the municipality located in northwestern Sonora. It was inaugurated by the governor of Sonora, Alfonso Durazo Montaño, and authorities of the National Water Commission (Conagua), led by Rodolfo Castro Valdez.

The first stage of the plant required an investment of 48,099,101 pesos and will have an initial treatment capacity of 40 liters per second, with a direct impact on 62,301 inhabitants of this coastal municipality, where pressure on the water system intensifies during high-tourism seasons and in the summer months.

According to information shared during the inauguration ceremony, the project includes a second stage with a planned investment of 70 million pesos, which will increase capacity by an additional 60 liters per second, to reach a total processing capacity of 100 liters.

The lack of wastewater treatment infrastructure has been one of the persistent problems in Puerto Peñasco, a tourist destination that during vacation periods considerably increases its demand for services, particularly drinking water supply and sanitation management.

During the same tour, the state governor led the handover of 88 outboard motors as part of the Outboard Motor Modernization Program for Small Vessels, aimed at fishing cooperatives and permit holders from Puerto Peñasco, San Luis Río Colorado and Caborca, with an investment of 15 million pesos. The region's fishing sector, one of the main economic activities of Sonora's northern coast, has for years demanded equipment to reduce operating costs and renew the vessels used in coastal fishing.
