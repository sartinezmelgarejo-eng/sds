This article by the La Jornada editorial staff originally appeared in the June 17, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

United States Vice President JD Vance stated that "we would take military action" in Mexico to combat drug trafficking cartels, although he added that "we would love to work with the Mexican government."

In an interview with N+ Univisión, which will air on Sunday, June 21, and from which excerpts were released this Wednesday, Vance emphasized that they have to reserve "the right" to bring assets into Mexican national territory.

When questioned by journalist Ilia Calderón about whether the administration of President Donald Trump will respect the "red line" of not conducting military operations on Mexican territory, which she attributed to the position of President Claudia Sheinbaum, Vance did not give a concrete answer. Instead, he reiterated that his priority is the defense of his country, although he insisted that he prefers to coordinate with the Mexican government.

"We want to collaborate with the Mexican government to make sure they can take action against the cartels themselves. The cartels ran rampant during the Biden administration because they had plenty of money. We want to help the Mexican government and the people of Mexico reduce the power of those cartels. … We would take military action if we considered it necessary to protect our people. We don't want to do it; but we have to reserve that right," Vance stated.

And he added that "let me give you a very concrete example: if there's a cartel that has a massive shipment of weapons and fentanyl about to enter the United States of America and the only way to stop it is to go after that cartel, of course we have to go after it. We would love to collaborate with the Mexican government, but we have to defend our own people," he concluded.
