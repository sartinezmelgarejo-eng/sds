This article by Reuters originally appeared in the July 6, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Washington. Toyota Motor Corp announced on Monday that it will build a new $3.6 billion automobile plant in Texas and will move part of its truck production from Mexico to the United States.

The Japanese manufacturer said the new building, of 2.5 million square feet (232,257.6 square meters), will be located at its San Antonio industrial complex and will begin operations in 2030, generating 2,000 jobs.

Toyota noted that, once construction is completed, it will move production of its mid-size Tacoma pickup from the Toyota Manufacturing Baja California plant, in Mexico, to the one in Texas.

The automaker will continue manufacturing Tacoma pickups at its Guanajuato plant, in Mexico. Toyota already produces Tundra pickups and SUVs at its current assembly plant in San Antonio, on the same site where the new factory will be built, and the opening of a new rear-axle plant of 46,450 square meters is planned for the fall.

U.S. President Donald Trump has pressured vehicle manufacturers to move a larger share of their production to the United States and has raised tariffs on automobiles, steel, aluminum, and components.

Toyota has affirmed that it maintains its commitment to its operations in Mexico, Canada, and the United States, and has urged Trump to extend the North American trade agreement, the USMCA, which, according to automakers, is fundamental to integrated vehicle production.

In 2020, Toyota moved production of the Tacoma from San Antonio to the Guanajuato plant, along with the Baja California plant, where the pickup had been manufactured since 2004.
