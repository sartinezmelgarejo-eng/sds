This article by Fernanda Monroy originally appeared in the July 28, 2026 edition of Contralínea, a Mexican investigative journalism magazine.

The conclusions of the group of experts analyzing the feasibility of using hydraulic fracturing (fracking) to extract natural gas and oil in Mexico will be ready within two weeks at the latest, President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo reported, explaining that the delivery of the report was delayed due to the expansion of the team of specialists in charge of preparing the assessment.

During her morning press conference, the president noted that the commission incorporated specialists who have historically expressed their rejection of the exploitation of unconventional gas, with the aim of broadening the technical debate over the use of fracking.

She also indicated that a second group of academics was brought in to develop energy planning scenarios toward 2035 and 2040, with an emphasis on alternatives that would allow reducing dependence on natural gas through other energy sources and energy efficiency measures.

"The commission was expanded with two additional points of view: people who have historically been against the exploitation of unconventional gas, and a group from different academic institutions that could draw up an energy planning framework considering other energy sources."

She assured that both groups already show significant progress, and have even reached agreements on most of the analysis. She specified that all that remains is to conclude the scenario on the growth of energy consumption and the way in which natural gas could be replaced by other energy sources.

The president estimated that the work could conclude during the coming week and that, within two weeks at the latest, the federal government will present the commission's conclusions and recommendations.

"They are going to be satisfactory conclusions because of the way the work has been done, in an interdisciplinary manner, to find the best framework for the future growth of the Mexican energy sector."

She added that the report will also raise the need to carry out new studies during this year to delve deeper into some aspects related to energy planning.

As part of that process, President Claudia Sheinbaum highlighted that the Ministry of Energy recently announced more ambitious goals to accelerate the energy transition, among them moving up to 2030 the objective of reaching 50 percent of electricity generation from renewable sources.
