This article by Néstor Jiménez and Alma E. Muñoz originally appeared in the July 30, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Federal authorities are maintaining a "temporary closure" in the area of the chemical spill caused by the derailment of a Ferromex freight train, in the municipality of Naco, Sonora, with the aim of verifying that there are no subsequent risks. At the same time, an analysis is being carried out to determine whether there was any impact on the aquifer, the head of the Secretariat of Environment and Natural Resources (Semarnat), Alicia Bárcena, stated on Thursday.

The federal official pointed out that the Federal Attorney's Office for Environmental Protection (Profepa) has been present along with Civil Protection at the site of this incident.

"A temporary closure was carried out in the area of the accident as a safety measure, in order to restrict access. An overflight was carried out of 5 kilometers of the streambed, including the village of El Sauz, which is located 3.7 kilometers from the site of the accident. That worried us a great deal, but no evidence of the spilled solution was identified," she detailed as she attended the morning press conference at the National Palace, led by President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo.

Bárcena recalled that the spill was of copper, as well as sodium hydrosulfide. She stressed that "it was confirmed that Ferromex has already contained the spill. It fell into a small stream, which is a seasonal stream, and Ferromex has already collected all the copper that had been spilled and is transferring 1,700 liters of sodium hydrosulfide, with the purpose of reducing the risk."

She added that technical analyses are being carried out "to see whether there was any permeability or leaching into the aquifer," mainly following the heavy rains that were recorded after the spill.

Meanwhile, the Secretary of the Environment referred to the projects assumed by the government of Mexico to clean up the Tijuana River, agreed within Minute 333 of the International Boundary and Water Commission. She affirmed that while Mexico has "faithfully" complied with the agreements, the United States still has pending actions.

Of around 38 projects committed to under that framework, there is progress on 17 and a decision by the government to invest in the cleanup of the Tijuana River, from where it originates to where it ends at the border with San Diego, as well as the expansion of a treatment plant.

"The truth is that we have faithfully complied with all the commitments we made in this Minute 333; it remains for the United States to comply with what they are doing at the PITAR treatment plant (International Wastewater Treatment Plant) that is on the United States side. They still owe us those portions," she emphasized.

She indicated that there are periodic meetings with the U.S. authorities as part of the International Boundary and Water Commission, and she highlighted that the United States has publicly recognized the efforts that have been made in Mexico for the cleanup of the Tijuana River.

"Of course, it is true that we have not achieved total cleanup, but this is going to take us all of this year and possibly the next. Next year I think we will already solve it in the case of the Tijuana River," she added.
