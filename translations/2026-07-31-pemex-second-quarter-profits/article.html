This article by Alejandro Alegría originally appeared in the July 31, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. In the second quarter of 2026, Petróleos Mexicanos (Pemex) obtained profits of 18.024 billion pesos, a figure that reversed part of the losses reported in January-March, the company reported this Friday in a filing sent to the Mexican Stock Exchange (BMV).

At the close of the first half of 2026, Pemex's financial debt stood at 77.5 billion dollars, an amount that represented a decrease of 9.1 percent compared to the close of 2025. With this, the debt continued its process of reduction as has been observed under the current federal administration.

“Pemex is advancing in what matters most for Mexico: it is maintaining a hydrocarbon production platform. It is processing more crude oil. It is generating more fuel. It is securing the national supply. It is showing progress in its financial performance and it is building strategic alliances to raise its long-term capacities. But above all, Pemex is fulfilling its central role: to be a productive, strategic company at the service of Mexico's energy sovereignty,” said the director general, Juan Carlos Carpio, on a call with investors and analysts.

Although in April-June Pemex obtained profits of 18.024 billion pesos, the amount showed a contraction of 69.71 percent compared to the profits of 59.516 billion pesos reported in the same period of 2025.

“This result is explained mainly by higher sales, a decrease in the impairment of fixed assets, and lower transportation, distribution, and sales expenses, as well as administrative expenses, effects partially offset by higher product purchases, higher taxes and duties, a higher cost from derivative financial instruments, and a lower foreign-exchange gain,” the company stated in the report.

In the first half of 2026, the company registered losses of 27.968 billion pesos, an amount that contrasts with the profits of 16.187 billion pesos reported in January-June of 2025.

In April-June, revenue from sales and services amounted to 510.4 billion pesos, which meant a year-on-year increase of 30.3 percent, explained by higher export sales derived from a higher price in the commercialization of crude oil and an increase in the volume of distillates, as well as by higher domestic sales of diesel, Magna gasoline, and jet fuel due to higher volumes and prices.

Carpio commented that in an international environment marked by energy volatility, political tensions, and adjustments in global flows of crude oil and petroleum products, Pemex's capacity to sustain stable production levels, raise crude processing, and increase fuel production takes on strategic relevance.

“The results observed in this quarter reflect not only a favorable operating performance, but also Pemex's capacity to contribute to the country's energy security, to the stability of the domestic market, and to compliance with the national energy policy,” he stated.

Pemex reported that total hydrocarbon production during the second quarter of 2026 averaged 2.447 million barrels per day, which represents an increase of 4.6 percent compared to the same period of the previous year.

Crude processing in refineries was 1.8 million barrels per day, which represented an increase of 3 percent compared to the 980 thousand barrels per day reported in April-June of last year.

Carpio emphasized that these levels are fundamental to strengthening energy security in the current political environment and depending less on external markets.

“Every additional barrel that we process in Mexico strengthens the national fuel supply, reduces vulnerabilities in the face of international disruptions, and contributes to greater energy resilience,” Carpio said.

Total production of petroleum products also advanced. In the second quarter of 2026 it was 1.029 million barrels per day, a figure that showed an increase of 3 percent compared to the 995 thousand reported in the same quarter of 2025. He highlighted that diesel production grew 19 percent year-on-year and that of gasolines 3 percent.

During the second quarter of 2026, the total charge for taxes and duties, including the Petroleum Duty for Well-Being, amounted to 70.3 billion pesos, compared to 47.3 billion pesos in the same period of 2025, mainly due to a higher price of oil used for the determination of hydrocarbon extraction duties.
