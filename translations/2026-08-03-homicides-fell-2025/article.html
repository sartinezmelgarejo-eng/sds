This article by Clara Zepeda originally appeared in the August 3, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

During 2025, 27,989 deaths from presumed homicide were registered in Mexico, which represented an annual decrease of 16 percent, with firearm shootings being the main method used to cause death, at 19,690 events, the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) revealed.

INEGI's data, which released this Monday the preliminary figures of the statistics on presumed homicides registered nationwide during 2025, estimate that homicides in the country dropped 16.6 percent compared to the definitive 2024 figures of 33,550 deaths. In addition, the 2025 figure reached the lowest number since 2016.

The Institute specified that, on a preliminary basis, a rate of 21.4 homicides per 100,000 inhabitants was obtained nationwide. In comparison with 2024, whose rate was calculated with definitive information, a decrease was observed, going from 25.8 to 21.4 per 100,000 inhabitants.

The main specified method used to cause death was firearm shooting, at 70.8 percent. Sharp weapons or objects ranked in second position, at 9.4 percent (2,621 homicides).

In 2025, the rate of presumed homicides among men was 38.4 per 100,000. With respect to 2024 (when the rate with definitive information was 46.4), a decrease of eight was observed. In the case of women, the rate was 4.7 presumed homicides per 100,000. This rate was lower than the one calculated with the definitive information from 2024, which was 5.6.

Guanajuato registered the highest number of presumed homicides nationwide, at 3,249; it was followed by the state of México, with 2,575 deaths; Chihuahua, with 2,106; and Baja California, with 2,095.

The state with the fewest presumed homicides in 2025 was Yucatán, with 42, which also showed a decline with respect to 2024 (65 cases); it was followed by Campeche, with 97, and Durango, with 108, both with slight drops.

Notably, Baja California Sur showed a significant upturn from one year to the next, going from 85 to 203 presumed homicides in 2024 and 2025. In Sinaloa it went from a count of 1,032 to 1,805 over the reference period.
