This article by David Castellanos Terán originally appeared in the August 5, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico’s premier left wing daily newspaper.

Tampico, Tam. With drones to disperse seeds in hard-to-reach areas of the Sierra Madre, Tamaulipas will contribute more than 15,000 trees during the remainder of 2026 to the national environmental restoration program.

The project, announced by Governor Américo Villarreal Anaya, seeks to expand reforestation coverage through aerial technology and to bring recovery actions to areas where terrain conditions make conventional work difficult.

“We have a project of more than 15,000 trees for the remainder of this year, but it is also a more ambitious program, in which seeds will be dispersed in the Sierra Madre by means of drones,” Villarreal said.

The strategy is part of the coordinated work between the Government of Tamaulipas and the Federation to recover affected areas and strengthen the state’s ecosystems.

The program will not be limited to the Sierra Madre. In the Laguna Madre, mangrove restoration actions continue, particularly of red mangrove and black mangrove.

In addition, this Sunday a reforestation day will be held in the vicinity of the San Antonio ejido, in Jaumave, where native species such as yucca, mesquite, ebony, and oak will be planted.

The governor did not yet detail the area that will be treated by drones, the number of seeds that will be dispersed, or the specific areas of the Sierra Madre where the program will begin.

Government Reshuffle

On another topic, Villarreal stated that the change of leadership at the Delegation of Welfare Programs will help strengthen the operation of federal support in Tamaulipas.

The governor highlighted the experience of María de Lourdes Puente Vizcarra, the new head of the agency, and later led a meeting with the state Welfare structure to review the attention given to people in conditions of extreme poverty.
