This article by Jessika Becerra originally appeared in the August 7, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico’s premier left-wing daily newspaper.

51.5 percent of the country’s cell phone lines must be linked to the Unique Population Registry Code (CURP) before the year ends, since this mandatory process has been completed for 48.5 percent of mobile numbers, according to the most recent data from the Telecommunications Regulatory Commission (CRT).

So far, 69,666,315 lines are linked to the CURP, of which 22.8 million operate under the postpaid model. There are 144 million lines in the country, so 74.3 million remain to be linked—that is, a little more than half.

The deadline for those ending in zero will be this coming August 15, and for those ending in 1 it will be the 31st. In turn, the deadline for those with prepaid lines ending in 2 will be September 15, and for those ending in 3 it will be the 30th, according to the calendar the CRT set on June 30, the date on which the registration deadline originally ended.

On that date the regulatory agency established staggered deadlines according to the last digit of cell phone numbers, with the aim that all lines be registered before December 31.

However, once the deadline for each digit passes, the phone companies—Telcel, AT&T, Movistar, and Altán Redes (in the case of Bait and other mobile virtual operators)—will suspend service within the following 72 hours, and only the seismic alert services will remain available, as well as 911, 079, 088, and 089, and calls to the companies that provide the service.

Lines can be reactivated if users register after their corresponding deadline.

Users of lines that are suspended could have trouble carrying out operations in mobile device applications such as banking apps, since these use text messages (SMS) for authentication purposes.

According to the telecommunications consultancy The Competitive Intelligence Unit (The CIU), the mobile market is in a phase of reordering due to the mandatory registration, or linking, of lines.

According to its estimates—based on the reports that cell phone companies submit to the Mexican Stock Exchange—the linking caused a cumulative contraction of 6.3 million prepaid lines in the first half of the year.

“At the close of the second quarter of 2026, the market’s total base adjusted to 157.1 million active lines, marking its second consecutive quarter with a reduction in the net volume of subscribers, after years of relentless growth,” the firm noted.

“With this, the cumulative loss of lines in the year of national line registration, or linking, is 4.9 million, going from 162 [million] in December 2025 to 157.1 [million] in June 2026,” said The CIU, whose figures differ from those of the CRT.
