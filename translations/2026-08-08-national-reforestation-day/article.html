This article originally appeared in the August 8, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. The Secretariat of Agriculture and Rural Development (Sader) called on the public to join tomorrow's National Reforestation Day 2026, which aims to plant 6.6 million trees and plants, from 302 species, across 32,184 hectares in the country's 32 states.

It explained that the effort will cover 621 municipalities and 1,632 agrarian centers, with the participation of 235,097 people.

"The joint effort will have a positive impact on temperate and cloud forests, humid and dry jungles, scrublands, grasslands, mangroves, 17 Protected Natural Areas (ANP), and 17 areas voluntarily set aside for conservation," it emphasized.

To find the reforestation sites in the 32 states, Sader set up the website https://www.gob.mx/agricultura/acciones-y-programas/jornada-nacional-de-reforestacion-2026 for volunteers and brigade members who wish to actively join the planting and environmental care work, which will begin at 7:00 a.m. Central Mexico time.

It noted that this activity will simultaneously unite the efforts of the federal, state and municipal governments, led by Sader; the Secretariats of Environment and Natural Resources (Semarnat) and of Welfare; the national commissions for Forestry (Conafor) and for Protected Natural Areas (Conanp); and Sembrando Vida.

"It is the permanent beginning of the conservation and restoration of different ecosystems and the strengthening of the country's agricultural and silvopastoral zones," it stated in a communiqué.

Sader noted that the National Reforestation Day follows up on what President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo proposed at the G20 Leaders' Summit in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil, in November 2024.

There, it recalled, she "proposed sowing peace and life, instead of wars, by allocating 1.0 percent of military spending to launch the largest reforestation program in history."

It indicated that adopting a tree and/or plant means not only planting it; it is necessary to water it, care for it and watch it grow, as part of the commitment to help improve air quality, reduce temperatures and build a better future for the new generations.

With the National Day, it stressed, the Mexican government "reaffirms its commitment to the restoration of ecosystems, soil conservation and the fight against climate change throughout the country."
