This article by José Shaddai Olvera Torres originally appeared in the August 10, 2026 edition of El Chamuco, a Mexican political magazine.

The new Utopía reclaims nearly 20,000 square meters of public space and will benefit more than 130,000 residents with health, culture, sports, care, and community services.

The head of government of Mexico City, Clara Brugada Molina, inaugurated the “Elena Poniatowska Amor” Utopía in the neighborhood of San Lucas, Coyoacán borough, a project that required an investment of more than 242 million pesos and made it possible to reclaim nearly 20,000 square meters of public space.

The new Utopía will directly benefit more than 130,000 residents of 17 neighborhoods and joins the network of community spaces promoted by the Mexico City government.

Brugada was accompanied by the writer and journalist Elena Poniatowska, cultural figures, and local and federal legislators.

Why Does the Utopía Bear the Name of Elena Poniatowska?

The head of government explained that the space received the writer’s name in recognition of her career in Mexican literature and her commitment to social causes, historical memory, and the defense of vulnerable sectors.

“We decided to call this Utopía Elena Poniatowska Amor, in recognition of one of the greatest Mexican writers of all time.”

Brugada noted that the Utopías are part of an urban transformation strategy based on four pillars: care, social urbanism, proximity, and environmental sustainability.

The goal, she explained, is to bring free, quality services closer to communities, strengthen the social fabric, and contribute to a more inclusive and democratic city.

Health, Care, and Community Attention

The Utopía will offer services aimed at different sectors of the population.

These include a dining hall, a laundry, Childcare and Development Centers, a Day Center for older adults, medical offices, dental care, mammography, physical rehabilitation, and specialized services for women.

There will also be care for people with disabilities and a Community Center for People with Parkinson’s.

“Here we will have this service as a public and free service, unique in Mexico City.”

The complex will also offer care for people experiencing violence, services for the sexually diverse population, mental health programs, and comprehensive attention for substance use.

Sports, Technology, and Training

Among the sports facilities are a semi-Olympic pool, a gym, and a sports pavilion, in addition to spaces for indoor sports, Tai Chi, yoga, and ping-pong.

The project also incorporates an employment agency and a digital transformation center with 150 computers and 3D printers, as well as a school of games and animation.

According to the Secretary of Works and Services, Raúl Basulto Luviano, the project transformed a plot of more than 9,000 square meters through the rehabilitation and construction of more than 18,000 square meters of public infrastructure.

A Utopía with an Environmental Focus

One of the spaces highlighted by Brugada is the Climate Action Room, designed to raise public awareness about the effects of climate change through immersive experiences, simulators, and interactive activities.

The Utopía will also have a tortillería that will use native corn produced on Mexico City’s conservation land, with the goal of strengthening the local economy and promoting sustainable food.

Museum and Cultural Spaces

The cultural dimension will occupy a central place in the new complex.

The Utopía will house the Museo Coyote, dedicated to contemporary and popular art, which will open with an immersive experience on the life and work of Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera.

The experience will include nearly 300 images, audiovisual resources, virtual reality, and interactive spaces aimed also at children and young people.

The venue will also have an auditorium for more than 40 people, three museum halls, four open-air cultural forums, a terrace, and spaces for artistic, musical, and literary training.

A Space for New Generations of Writers

Brugada stressed that the Utopía will seek to especially encourage the participation of young people and women interested in literary creation.

“We have thought of this Utopía Elena Poniatowska Amor as a space for young people. In particular, for young women who want to begin in the noble craft of literature. Elena Poniatowska taught us that literature is not apart from history. It can enter into it, accompany its struggles, and rescue the voices that power sought to blur.”

The mayor also vindicated Poniatowska’s career and affirmed that her work will endure for the coming generations of Mexicans as part of the country’s contemporary history.

Poniatowska Celebrates the Inauguration

During the event, Elena Poniatowska expressed her joy at the inauguration of the first Utopía in Coyoacán to bear her name.

“It is very nice to see you all here. To be here in this beautiful and solid place, strong, that will carry us forward, that will protect us, that will care for us; and, moreover, to which we can give what we are, what we hold in our hearts, our work, our hopes, and everything we also wish for our descendants.”

The journalist and producer Epigmenio Ibarra recognized the process of transformation that, in his view, is underway in the country, and stated that “even utopias become reality.”

For his part, Mauricio Ocampo, a resident of the San Lucas neighborhood, called on the population to make use of the facilities, take care of them, and keep them clean.

The new Utopía thus joins Mexico City’s strategy of community public spaces, with an offering that combines care, health, sports, culture, technology, social attention, and environmental education.
