This article by Álvaro Delgado Gómez originally appeared in the August 11, 2026 edition of SinEmbargo.

Mexico City, August 11 (SinEmbargo).— Once again, as he has done on several occasions since Donald Trump took office, the president of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI), Alejandro Moreno Cárdenas, "Alito," requested the intervention of the United States government in Mexico to impose "economic sanctions" and revoke the visas of the National Electoral Institute (INE) councilors who unanimously ordered him to stop calling Morena a "narco-party" and to remove from his social media the posts in which he uses that label.

"Alito" Moreno Cárdenas has repeatedly turned to the United States to bring his grievances against the Mexican government. Since February 2025, for example, he has traveled on various occasions to Washington, where he has claimed to hold meetings with US political figures and has denounced an alleged political persecution in Mexico.

"Alito" was in the US capital in August and September of that year—when he met with Republican congresswoman María Elvira Salazar—and again in February and May of 2026, when he reiterated his accusations against Morena. Now he once again turns to the US authorities, this time to request sanctions against INE councilors.

"Alito" Calls for Sanctioning the INE

Moreno Cárdenas announced that this Monday he filed a complaint with the United States Department of State to request that the actions of councilors Rita Bell López Vences, Frida Denisse Gómez Puga, and Arturo Castillo Loza be reviewed and that they be sanctioned under the Global Magnitsky Human Rights Accountability Act.
