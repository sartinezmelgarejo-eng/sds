This article by Fernanda Monroy originally appeared in the August 12, 2026 edition of Contralínea, a Mexican investigative magazine.

Two Mexican Air Force transport planes took off in the early morning bound for Pereira, Colombia, loaded with 854 food parcels equivalent to 19.5 tons of non-perishable food for the population affected by the magnitude 7.4 earthquake that occurred this past August 10, the Secretariat of National Defense reported.

The flights departed at 7:11 and 7:40 a.m. on this August 12 from the Santa Lucía Military Air Base, in the State of Mexico, with 19 members of the Army, the Mexican Air Force (FAM), and the National Guard (GN) aboard.

According to the agency's statement, this first shipment is only part of the committed support, since a total of 2,562 food parcels equivalent to 58.5 tons of food will be transported through three logistical flights scheduled for August 12, 13, and 14.

Likewise, the Secretariat noted that it is keeping the Cali Humanitarian Aid grouping on alert, made up of 255 specialized military personnel from the Army, the FAM, and the GN, along with 20 canine teams, basic units formed by a soldier and a dog that work together as a team, ready to deploy to Colombia if the situation requires it.

Separately, the agency noted that the Military Strategic Collection Center is keeping in reserve bottled water, medicines, tools, and first-aid backpacks, among other supplies.

The Mexican government lamented the loss of life and material damage left by the earthquake, and expressed its wishes for a swift recovery for those injured. It also reaffirmed the Mexican people's tradition of solidarity with the peoples of Latin America and the world.
