This article by Gustavo Castillo García originally appeared in the August 12, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. A federal judge ordered that Guadalupe Hernández Hinojosa, legal representative of Ingemar, be bound over for trial on charges of hydrocarbon smuggling and organized crime; the company's majority shareholder is former Baja California governor Ernesto Ruffo Appel, and it is linked to a network for the illegal transfer of fuel from the United States into national territory by rail tankers.

According to the evidence gathered by the Attorney General's Office (FGR) during the investigation, Hernández Hinojosa allegedly took part in foreign-trade operations through which hydrocarbon volumes significantly lower than those actually transported were declared. She also allegedly participated in various legal and commercial acts related to the company's operation, including the formalization of contracts tied to the distribution of hydrocarbons, in addition to carrying out procedures before customs authorities to allow the entry of the illicit shipments.

Ingemar's legal representative was identified as an authorized signatory on several of the company's bank accounts, a circumstance that would have allowed her to take part in its financial administration, according to FGR reports. The evidence presented before a judge of the Federal Criminal Justice Center based in Almoloya de Juárez, State of Mexico, indicates that Hernández Hinojosa carried out procedures before customs authorities so that the irregularly imported fuel shipments would be released and regularized.

Hernández Hinojosa, who was arrested in recent days in the Polanco Chapultepec neighborhood of the Miguel Hidalgo borough of Mexico City, allegedly participated in the criminal structure through her corporate representation, financial administration, and the execution of legal acts linked to Ingemar's commercial operation. The Specialized Prosecutor's Office for Organized Crime (FEMDO) succeeded in having the federal judge order mandatory pretrial detention against the accused, meaning she must remain held at the Nezahualcóyotl Penitentiary and Social Reintegration Center.
