This article by Fernanda Monroy originally appeared in the August 12, 2026 edition of Contralínea, a Mexican investigative magazine.

Twelve years after the spill that Grupo México caused in the Bacanuchi and Sonora rivers, the Secretariat of the Environment and Natural Resources (Semarnat) presented this Wednesday in Ures, Sonora, the second phase of the basin's remediation program, focused on restoring soils, monitoring water quality, and addressing the effects that persist in the region's communities.

The head of the agency, Alicia Bárcena Ibarra, attended the presentation, accompanied by Governor Alfonso Durazo Montaño, as well as representatives of the Federal Attorney's Office for Environmental Protection (Profepa), the National Water Commission (Conagua), and units specializing in environmental regulation and hazardous materials management.

The origin of this new phase dates back to August 6, 2014, when nearly 40 million liters of acidulated copper sulfate from the Buenavista del Cobre mine, operated by Grupo México in Cananea, reached the Bacanuchi River and from there the Sonora River. The contamination affected a basin comprising eight municipalities and home to around 22,000 people.

"We have worked in recent months to bring to fruition the demands and needs that you, the residents of the Sonora River, have expressed to us," said the secretary, who insisted that the process seeks to advance the remediation without losing direct communication with the communities: "you are not alone, we come to listen to you and inform you with full transparency."

This second phase comes after the diagnostic work carried out throughout the year: in February, Semarnat personnel toured various points of the basin, among them Cananea, Bacoachi, Arizpe, Aconchi, and Ures, to take soil and sediment samples with X-ray fluorescence equipment, in order to identify the areas requiring deeper intervention.

The program is part of the Justice Plan for Cananea and the Sonora River, announced at the end of 2025 with a fund of 2,222.6 million pesos, of which 1,500 million correspond to Grupo México, 483.6 million to the federal government, and 180 million to the Sonora state government.

Those resources are earmarked for environmental studies, remediation of contaminated soils, permanent monitoring of water quality, and new hydraulic works, among them 16 water treatment plants, 16 disinfection systems, and upgrades to four plants already in operation.

The secretary acknowledged that there are still pending commitments to the basin's communities, for whom access to water remains one of the main demands. However, she said that Semarnat will closely follow up with Conagua. "We are going to be on top of them about those water treatment plants that were offered and promised, so that they make the delivery we have committed to."
