These letters to the editor originally appeared in the August 13, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

100 Years Since the Birth of Fidel Castro

What more can be said about Comandante Fidel Castro Ruz on the hundredth anniversary of his birth, on August 13, 1926, in Birán, Oriente (today Holguín), Cuba. From the assault on the Moncada barracks on July 26, 1953, when he founded the movement that bears its name, through the defeat and the trial (1955), at whose close Fidel delivered one of the most forceful phrases in history: "Condemn me, it does not matter, history will absolve me." And indeed, the peoples and the dispossessed of the world absolved him. Aboard the Granma, in 1956 from Veracruz, arrival and betrayal upon reaching Cuba. "How many weapons do we have?" the comandante asked. "Six," someone answered. "We've already won," Fidel replied. The rebel army, with its leader at the head, and other figures such as Camilo Cienfuegos, Ernesto Guevara, Raúl Castro…, took power on January 1, 1959. Thousands of kilometers away, in faraway Argentina, the undersigned can affirm that since his childhood, the triumphant Cuban Revolution helped him develop a liberating conception of the world and of society. On May 1, 2006, he had the privilege of attending, in Havana's Plaza de la Revolución, Fidel's last public address to his people. The revolution will survive all the empire's criminal attempts to destroy it. Together with the Cuban people and Fidel, we repeat: "Homeland or death, we will win."

— Miguel Socolovsky

A Beacon That Remains a Guide on the Centennial of His Birth

A child was born 100 years ago, on an August 13, on a Caribbean island of special beauty; a jewel that the great powers have always coveted. The Spanish invasion exterminated the original population; later, millions of people were kidnapped in Africa and brought there as slaves. In a war, a new empire seized Cuba from Spain. It turned the island into a center for its mafias and into a brothel, while the people suffered illiteracy and extreme misery. That child, named Fidel, grew up agile, strong, intelligent and generous. He devoted his energies to fighting for his land and his people and made a revolution that shook the world and awakened the yearning for freedom throughout Latin America and the Caribbean. That child died at the age of 90 without ever ceasing to be restless and rebellious. He defied the empire, which does not tolerate the dignity of the people and which today takes cruel revenge against that generous and brave people. Cuba, even while suffering a genocidal blockade, developed an exemplary system of health, education, sport and science, which it shared with the world with great solidarity, always supporting the peoples most in need and the victims of all kinds of disasters. The light that was lit 100 years ago is a beacon that guides us today.

— Pablo Moctezuma Barragán

Cuba's Revolutionary Leader Does Not Need to Be Vindicated

Yesterday, in La Jornada, an excellent article by Luis Hernández Navarro appeared about the commemoration, held in Havana, of the centenary of Fidel Castro. Unfortunately, the piece hangs from a headline that clashes with the newspaper's editorial line: "China and Russia Vindicate Fidel Castro Ruz on His Centenary." For the academic lexicon, to vindicate [reivindicar] means "to claim something to which one believes one has a right." The dictionary of doña María Moliner is more precise, since it defines the verb as "for someone to claim or defend a certain right of which they have been, or are threatened with being, dispossessed." Since the gringos are bent on discrediting the Cuban leader and, along with him, Cuba, which they keep strangled, it is worth clarifying that Fidel does not need to be vindicated. He is already in history, even though every day Marco Rubio and his cronies sing praises to Trump's aggressions against the Greater Antilles and spew stupidity about the heroic Cuban people.

— Humberto Musacchio

The PRI and the Violence of One Who Has Already Lost Everything

The PRI of Alejandro "Alito" Moreno and his pawns, such as Carlos Gutiérrez Mancilla, has become a sad caricature of what it once was. Today they are a tiny party, with no people, no members and completely forgotten by the public. Yet its agony is not silent: it has turned violent. Lacking ideas, proposals and any real project for Mexico, their only strategy is scandal. When people hear them, they think of the same thing: corruption, theft, fraud and lies. They know they are the public enemy of popular memory and that no one wants them. That frustration of knowing themselves to be losers leads them to resort to violence — not only the shoving and insults in Congress, but a daily psychological violence. They lie to our faces every day, trying to manipulate reality to make us believe they still matter. They want to sow fear and chaos because they have nothing else to offer. The cantina thug who throws punches when he runs out of arguments is the exact reflection of today's PRI. Their arrogance has run out and all that remains is the tantrum. The people have already decided: the PRI not only lost at the ballot box, it lost respect.

— Carlos Fernández Cárdenas
