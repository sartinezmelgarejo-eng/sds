This article by Néstor Jiménez originally appeared in the August 17, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Since 2020, the national leader of the Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI), Alejandro Moreno Cárdenas, has made at least 10 trips to the United States, mostly to file complaints against the federal government and Morena during meetings with members of the US government, as well as legislators and figures from that country's right wing.

At the same time, the party has grown smaller. In a little under a year, it lost almost 9 percent of its membership rolls, going from 894,220 members last October to 819,107 this month.

Although the tricolor has not specified the exact dates on which Moreno Cárdenas has traveled to the neighboring country, according to his social media posts and party leadership statements, the party leader has spent a total of at least 29 days in that country over the last six years.

During that period he met with Luis Almagro, former secretary general of the Organization of American States (OAS); with Republican congresswoman María Elvira Salazar, who has criticized Claudia Sheinbaum's government for supporting Cuba; with other members of Congress, as well as with members of the State Department and security agencies of that country.

This year, Alito Moreno made a tour of at least three days through the United States to denounce what he has called a "narco-state." He later returned to that country to file a complaint against members of the National Electoral Institute (INE).

Amid a growing offensive by the tricolor against Morena and the governments that emerged from that party, which, as elections approach, has raised the level of insults and accusations, he has also increased his spending on social media advertising.

For this year, the PRI allocated 16,288,123 pesos to "the contracting of advertising space on social media," through an agreement with the company Veintiuno Doce Consultoría Estratégica, which last year it paid 20,880,000 pesos for the creation, design, planning, and execution of advertising campaigns.

In addition, the PRI leader has also constantly resorted to his position as president of the Permanent Conference of Political Parties of Latin America (Copppal), which brings together 75 parties from 30 countries, as a platform to launch criticisms against the federal government and against what he has termed "authoritarian governments" on the continent.

Despite the tone of his speeches, the leader of the Labor Party (PT), Alberto Anaya, was named one of the group's vice presidents, while Vania Ávila, of Movimiento Ciudadano, was designated a member of it during its assembly, held last month.
