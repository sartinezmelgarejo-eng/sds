This article by Anahí Del Ángel originally appeared in the August 18, 2026 edition of Contralínea, an independent Mexican investigative journalism magazine.

During the 2026 International Drug Control Conference, held in Buenos Aires, Argentina, the Secretary of Security and Citizen Protection, Omar García Harfuch, highlighted the decrease of 51 percent in intentional homicides in Mexico, going from 86 cases in September 2024 to 42 in July 2026.

Before authorities and security commanders from various countries, the official attributed the results to the National Security Strategy promoted by President Claudia Sheinbaum, which is based on four pillars: attention to the causes, consolidation of the National Guard, strengthening of intelligence and investigation, and coordination among institutions and the three levels of government. He also added that high-impact crimes have decreased by 33 percent.

In giving his opening message, he thanked Terrance Cole, head of the United States Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA), stating that "since his arrival information has flowed much faster; we have had requests, at times for informal information, that helps us operate much faster, achieve better results, and obtain information on criminals who operate in our two countries, in the United States and Mexico, and in other parts of Latin America."

In addition, he indicated that since the start of the current administration more than 63,500 people have been arrested for high-impact crimes; more than 32,800 firearms and 518 tons of drugs were seized, among them more than two tons of fentanyl and more than 5 million pills of this substance. More than 2,700 clandestine laboratories were also dismantled.

The Secretary noted that the Army and the Navy seized 3.8 million liters and more than 932,000 kilograms of substances and chemical precursors used for the production of methamphetamine.

Likewise, the Secretariat of the Navy seized nearly 88 tons of cocaine during the administration, including an operation off the coast of Michoacán in which more than eight tons were seized, considered the largest maritime seizure of this drug carried out in a single operation by the institution.

García Harfuch indicated that the National Intelligence Center increased its force by 36 percent and currently has five Regional Intelligence Fusion Centers, 32 stations, and institutional presence in nine countries.

He added that, in financial matters, the Financial Intelligence Unit has added more than 2,300 people to the Blocked Persons List, immobilized more than 8.6 billion pesos, and blocked more than 24,600 accounts.

The official also highlighted Operation Northern Border, deployed since February 2025, with more than 11,000 troops on a permanent basis. As a result, he reported more than 17,000 arrests, as well as the seizure of more than 11,400 firearms, more than 2 million rounds of ammunition, and 176 tons of drugs.

He reported that Mexico also maintains 155 arrest warrants for extradition purposes related to investigations by U.S. agencies; in contrast, he noted that Mexican authorities have arrested 14 people wanted or considered priority targets by the FBI, linked to investigations for drug trafficking, homicide, money laundering, human trafficking, and other transnational criminal activities.

He stressed that cooperation with the United States is based on respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity, shared and differentiated responsibility, mutual trust, and coordination without subordination. For this reason, he called for strengthening the exchange of information and international coordination, warning that "no criminal organization can be stronger than States when they act together with intelligence and sustainable commitment."
