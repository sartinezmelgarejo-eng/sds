This article by Clara Zepeda originally appeared in the August 26, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. In the second quarter of 2026, at the national level, the percentage of the population in Mexico in labor poverty —whose labor income is below the value of the food basket— was 31.9 percent, which represented an annual decrease of 3.2 percentage points, the National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI) reported.

However, after reaching in the first quarter its lowest level in more than two decades, at 30.7 percent, the income a person receives for their work and that is not enough to buy their household’s basic food basket (labor poverty) rose in quarterly terms.

INEGI specified that the percentage of the population in a situation of labor poverty at the national level went from 35.1 to 31.9 percent over 12 months; while with respect to the previous quarter it went from 30.7 to 31.9 percent.

By area of residence, the population in a situation of labor poverty in the rural area decreased 4.4 percentage points annually, going from 49.1 to 44.7 percent. For its part, in the urban area it decreased 2.5 percentage points, from 30.8 to 28.3 percent.

With respect to the January-March period, labor poverty rose in the second quarter, at the national, urban, and rural levels alike. At the national level, labor poverty presented an increase of 1.2 percentage points with respect to the first quarter of 2026. For the rural and urban areas there were also increases in the same period; these were 0.5 and 1.4 percentage points, respectively.

The federal entities with the highest percentage of the population in a situation of labor poverty during the second quarter of 2026 were Chiapas, Oaxaca, and Guerrero, with 59.8, 52.9, and 52.2, respectively, generally the states with the highest levels of population employed in informality.

Those with the lowest percentage were Baja California Sur, Baja California, and Colima, with 13.8, 17.3, and 19.7, in that order.

Labor Income

In the second quarter of 2026, the average real labor income of the employed population at the national level was 8,029.42 pesos a month. Employed women reported a real labor income of 7,033.37 pesos, while men reported one of 8,734.06 pesos.

In the same period, the real per capita labor income was 3,655.63 pesos and presented an annual increase of 8 percent.

In the rural area an increase of 10.7 percent was observed, as it went from 1,924.5 to 2,129.51 pesos a month. The quarterly variation of the real per capita labor income presented an increase at the national level and also in the rural area, while in the urban area (4,085.87 pesos) a decrease was observed. The percentages were 0.1 at the national level and 1.7 in the rural area, while in the urban area the decrease was 0.3 percent.

As for the real per capita labor income, in 25 of the 32 federal entities an annual increase was recorded. The three largest increases occurred in Morelos (30.5 percent); the State of Mexico (20.9 percent); and San Luis Potosí (20.3 percent). Those that presented the largest fall in labor income in this period were Sinaloa (3.4 percent); Chihuahua (3.3 percent); and Quintana Roo (2.4 percent).

In the quarterly variation, 19 of the 32 federal entities recorded an increase. The most pronounced increases were observed in Yucatán (5.7 percent); Oaxaca (4.9 percent); and Guanajuato (4.2 percent); while the main reductions in labor income occurred in Michoacán (10.1 percent); Tamaulipas (7.3 percent); and Guerrero (6.5 percent).

The formally employed population reported a monthly labor income of 11,043.63 pesos and the informally employed population, 5,706.91 pesos. The real monthly labor income of the formally employed population presented an annual increase of 2.1 percent, while the income of the informally employed population grew 8.5 percent. As for the quarterly variation, the income of the formally and informally employed population decreased 1 percent and 0.8 percent, in that order.

For its part, the real wage bill —which is the total sum of the labor incomes of employed people— increased 18.3 percent, going from 374,281.33 to 442,868.16 million pesos. The foregoing represented an increase of 68,586.83 million pesos in this period. The quarterly variation presented a decrease of 0.2 percent.
