This editorial originally appeared in the September 3, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

The former president of Colombia, Gustavo Petro, is visiting Mexico, where he was received at the National Palace by President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo. He met with the community of his fellow citizens in our country and delivered the keynote lecture "Capital in the 21st Century: The Political Economy of the Climate Crisis" at the Alfonso Caso auditorium of Ciudad Universitaria, among other activities. He also granted this newspaper his first interview since he left the Casa de Nariño this past August 7.

During his visit to the head of the Executive, the man who has led Colombia's partisan left in recent decades warned that the region's new political reality includes the systematic interference of networks directed from Washington in Latin America's electoral processes. He cautioned that Mexico must watch what happens in the region's elections, such as the one that will take place in Brazil in a few weeks, as well as the advance of right-wing forces ahead of Mexico's 2027 midterm elections and the 2030 presidential election.

The former Colombian president elaborated on this challenge. "We live in a planetary shell, a matrix of lies in which bot farms seek to degrade political debate and drag it into insult. The intention of that Matrix is to enslave the human being with chains that are not only physical but also mental; for example, to enslave the human being in order to allow the exploitation of non-renewable natural resources."

He also recounted how close a US military operation came to being carried out on Colombian soil, similar to the one that led to the kidnapping of President Nicolás Maduro in neighboring Venezuela. In describing the sense of an imminent attack after Donald Trump pointed to him as "the next one" following the criminal intervention in Caracas, Petro reveals the most worrying aspect of the Republican administration: despite the omnipotence the magnate boasts of, in practice the hyperfascist clique that surrounds him often operates behind his back and hides some of the most questionable decisions from him. For the seasoned South American politician, several people in the White House are worse and more dangerous than Trump himself, who "is no longer entirely in command."

Petro's admonitions gain value insofar as they explain a central factor in the shift to the far right that Latin America is experiencing—including, tragically, Colombia itself—and they constitute the testimony of someone who experienced firsthand the onslaught of transnational networks of disinformation and manipulation. To gauge the virulence of the smear campaign concocted by the corporate media against the center-left leader, it is enough to say that the intensity of the insults and the shamelessness of the lies exceed the machinery built by the Mexican right against former president Andrés Manuel López Obrador. As here, the hatred is personal, but the consequences are not: the anti-Petrismo induced by the owners of big capital and their mouthpieces in the media paved the way for a government of and for the oligarchy.

Petro's contribution to political debate amounts to a call to redouble vigilance regarding foreign agents who take advantage of communication tools such as social media to install neofascist discourses, spread hoaxes, stir up hatred, and manipulate electorates in order to favor their external and internal sponsors—that is, the White House and a sector of big business hostile to democracy.

Alongside the bleak panorama he outlines, there is a message of hope: in his country, in 2022 the people elected the first progressive government in its history, and that experience cannot be seen as a failure. Societies are not defenseless against technofascism, he asserts, since they can protect themselves with strong minds and with the same resource as the oppressors: mathematics. Education is fundamental to the resistance that he himself joins and invites others to join.
