This article by Braulio Carbajal originally appeared in the September 3, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico’s premier left-wing daily newspaper.

In July, Mexico posted the largest goods trade surplus with the United States among its major trading partners, at US$26.31 billion, the Census Bureau of that country’s Commerce Department reported on Thursday. Over the same period, Mexico remained the leading trading partner of the U.S. economy and reached record figures in its exports to that market.

According to the monthly report, the Mexican trade surplus resulted from unprecedented exports of US$60.55 billion, a figure that represented a year-over-year increase of 33.6 percent, as well as imports of US$34.24 billion, an increase of 18.3 percent.

With that result, Mexico surpassed Vietnam, which in July accumulated a surplus of US$24.83 billion; Taiwan, with US$20.666 billion; and China, with US$17.361 billion. In all three cases, exports to the U.S. market were considerably smaller than Mexico’s. China’s sales to the United States totaled US$9.71 billion, Taiwan’s US$5.001 billion, and Vietnam’s just US$1.589 billion.

The result comes at a time when the review of the Treaty Between Mexico, the United States, and Canada (T-MEC) is under way.

This past July, Washington chose not to automatically renew the agreement in its current form, and the instrument entered a process of annual reviews that will extend until 2036. Despite this, the treaty remains in force and Mexico continues to enjoy its trade benefits. One of the issues the U.S. president, Donald Trump, has most criticized is precisely the high trade deficit the U.S. maintains with Mexico.
