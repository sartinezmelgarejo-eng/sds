This article by Chen Daojiang, China’s Ambassador to Mexico, originally appeared in the September 4, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico’s premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Nearly 500 years ago, the “China Nao,” laden with silk and porcelain, arrived in Acapulco, an event that turned the Pacific Ocean from an abyss separating China and Mexico into the bond that unites them. As a Latin American saying goes: “old friend, aged wine.” The friendship between China and Mexico is precisely this wine, which, thanks to efforts passed down from generation to generation, has grown ever more beautiful.

Political mutual trust steers the direction of our friendship. In 1972, China and Mexico established diplomatic relations. To this day the elms remain lush along that avenue in Mexico City bearing the name of Chinese Premier Zhou Enlai. In 2013, President Xi Jinping made a historic visit to Mexico, and the two countries elevated their relations to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. In 2023 and 2024, President Xi Jinping met, respectively, with President López Obrador and with President Claudia Sheinbaum, charting the course for the development of our bilateral ties. Under the guidance of both countries’ heads of state, exchanges among governments, legislative bodies, political parties, and local authorities have become ever more frequent, drawing our bonds still closer. In two days, Foreign Relations Secretary Velasco will make his first visit to China as Mexico’s Foreign Minister, adding a new chapter to the exchanges between the two nations. The great man of letters Octavio Paz said: “Friendship erases time.” Over more than half a century, each of the interactions between China and Mexico has driven the unfolding of our relations, allowing our mutual trust to remain firm and solid in the face of international vicissitudes.

Practical cooperation lays the foundations of our friendship. The volume of bilateral trade has surpassed US$100 billion for several consecutive years, and China has consolidated its position as Mexico’s second-largest trading partner. More than 200 Chinese companies are investing and doing business in Mexico, generating hundreds of thousands of local jobs and increasing the competitiveness of “Made in Mexico.” Despite the complex and changing external environment, Sino-Mexican trade has shown remarkable resilience. Every day more Mexican products, such as avocados, blueberries, and pork, reach Chinese homes. At present, China is steadily advancing the high-quality implementation of its 15th Five-Year Plan, and Mexico is decisively promoting “Plan México.” There is enormous potential for cooperation in areas such as industrial upgrading, the green transition, and digital innovation. China encourages its companies to join “Plan México,” and welcomes more quality Mexican products into its market. The sacred Maya book Popol Vuh reads: “Let all rise, let no one be left behind.” This is precisely the original aspiration of Sino-Mexican cooperation: mutual benefit and shared gains.

Cultural and people-to-people exchange builds bridges for our friendship. China and Mexico are both millennia-old civilizations, and that historical affinity naturally brings their peoples closer. This past February, more than 50,000 people celebrated together in Mexico City the Chinese New Year of the Horse, with traditional dragon and lion dances to the rhythm of traditional Mexican music. The Day of the Dead and tequila, symbols of Mexico, are gaining ever more popularity in China. In June, the World Cup opened at the Azteca Stadium, whose remodeling included the participation of Chinese companies, and the light rail that leads directly to the stadium also bears the seal of “Made in China.” As for diving, Chinese coaches have led the Mexican team for years, and the athletes of both countries are both rivals and dear friends. The two direct flights between China and Mexico show what “friendship erases geographic distance” means.

An Aztec poet wrote: “My flowers will not end, my songs will not cease.” The dialogue between the Chinese and Mexican civilizations that has spanned five centuries brims with new dynamism. This coming November, China will host the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders’ Meeting in Shenzhen, and in 2028 Mexico will host the same event. As two large developing countries and important members of the Global South, China and Mexico advance multilateralism shoulder to shoulder and lead regional cooperation, thereby contributing “Asia-Pacific strength” to global economic growth. As Confucius said: “If you wish to stand, help others to stand; if you wish to prosper, help others to prosper.” For his part, former president Benito Juárez proclaimed: “Respect for the rights of others is peace.” The wise of both nations agree on one and the same truth: only through mutual achievement is shared development attained. After more than 50 years of sincere friendship and mutual understanding, the wine of Sino-Mexican friendship is already an exquisite vintage, one that will grow even more aromatic with the passage of time.
