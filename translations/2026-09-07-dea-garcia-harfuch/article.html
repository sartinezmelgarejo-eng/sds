This article by José Shaddai Olvera Torres originally appeared in the September 7, 2026 edition of El Chamuco y los Hijos del Averno, a Mexican satirical and political magazine.

The United States Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) claimed a decisive role in the recent methamphetamine seizures carried out in Mexico, including the seizure of more than 16 tons in Sinaloa. Its version, however, contradicts the report of the Secretary of Security and Citizen Protection, Omar García Harfuch, who stated that the operation arose from the intelligence work of Mexican institutions.

On its social media, the DEA claimed that the seizures "would not have happened" without its intelligence and its investigations. It also maintained that, in a little over a week, Mexican authorities dismantled eight significant methamphetamine laboratories with its collaboration.

The US statement does not merely acknowledge an exchange of information: it presents the DEA's intervention as an indispensable condition for the success of the operations. In doing so, the agency pushes into the background the work of the Mexican forces that planned and executed the actions on national territory.

García Harfuch's version establishes a different hierarchy. The official explained that the result was the product of the Naval Intelligence Unit, whose work was strengthened by information shared by US agencies. That is, Mexico led the investigation and the United States played a collaborative role.

The clandestine complex located in Sinaloa had an area of 5,700 square meters and was the second-largest methamphetamine laboratory discovered during the government of Claudia Sheinbaum. The operation was led by the Navy Secretariat, with the participation of the Secretariat of Security and Citizen Protection and the Attorney General's Office.

In addition to the 16 tons of methamphetamine, more than 10 tons and thousands of liters of chemical substances used to manufacture the drug were seized. García Harfuch estimated that the seizure caused a loss of approximately 347 million dollars to the criminal organization involved.

The difference between the two versions raises questions about the real scope of US participation and about the DEA's interest in publicly capitalizing on an operation executed by Mexican authorities. While the agency claims the decisive intelligence for itself, the Mexican government maintains that the cooperation was complementary and subordinate to a national investigation.

García Harfuch insisted that international collaboration will continue "with full respect" for sovereignty. Nonetheless, the DEA's message reveals the tensions that persist in the bilateral relationship: the United States presents the operation as the result of its intervention, while Mexico claims it as its own action supported by the exchange of information.
