This article by Anahí Del Ángel originally appeared in the September 8, 2026 edition of Contralínea, an independent Mexican investigative magazine.

Mexico’s Congress could carry out a review to verify whether Fernando Cerimedo had influence or impact on Mexico’s electoral processes, and by what mechanisms, said President Claudia Sheinbaum, after the government of Chile announced the creation of a commission to investigate his possible intervention in that country’s political processes.

“There is no commission or special oversight; it wouldn’t hurt; perhaps Congress could review whether they have had any influence or not, and under what conditions, because what we have is that they themselves, that is, Cerimedo and [Javier] Negre, the people from La Derecha Diario, admit that they were influencing social media against the president of Mexico,” said the head of the federal Executive.

In this regard, she recalled the statements by the lawyer Nadia Beller, Cerimedo’s wife, who, after suffering an armed attack, reportedly said that the Argentine consultant had said he “was going to finish off President Sheinbaum,” an expression that, she clarified, was reportedly used in a political sense.

She also referred to the public statements by Javier Negre, a member of La Derecha Diario, who claimed that “[Ricardo] Salinas Pliego hired him” to carry out activities in Mexico, and who later said he would leave the country; “he said it didn’t go very well for him and that’s why he was leaving Mexico, by the way.”

The president said the importance of debating the issue is based on the use of financial resources and social media to try to influence public opinion, “above all, [with] fake news that they seek to make go viral, which is what is extremely serious.”

As a recent example of how these campaigns work, she mentioned the video circulated about an assembly held in Colima, in the context of her national tour for her Second Government Report, in which attendees at the event are heard shouting “get out, get out,” with which they tried to push the idea that the shouts were directed at the president when in reality they were against a woman who was trying to interrupt the report.

“They presented it in such a way, with a lie, even a journalist posted it on her social media, that ‘they shouted at the president: get out, get out,’ when that is totally false,” she maintained.

Based on this case, she questioned the capacity of digital platforms to amplify a false version through algorithms and content generated or spread with artificial intelligence and with the help of bot farms.

She explained that when a person interacts with a post, recommendation systems can repeatedly show them similar content, which can contribute to a false claim taking on the appearance of reality through repetition.
