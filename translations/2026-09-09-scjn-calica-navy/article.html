This article by Iván Evair Saldaña originally appeared in the September 9, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico’s premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. The Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation (SCJN) on Wednesday denied an injunction to Calizas Industriales del Carmen (Calica), a subsidiary of the U.S. company Vulcan Materials, upholding the powers of the Mexican Navy to carry out Coast Guard functions along coasts, marine zones, and port facilities.

By unanimous vote, the Full Court approved the draft ruling by Justice Irving Espinosa Betanzo, which declared constitutional Article 2, section V, subsection a) of the Organic Law of the Mexican Navy, which empowers the naval institution to carry out surveillance, verification, visitation, and inspection tasks in those areas.

Calica challenged the rule based on a May 2023 official letter through which Navy personnel required it to grant access to its surveillance cameras, as well as the username and password, of the port terminal it operates at Punta Venado, Quintana Roo.

The Court denied the injunction against the law and returned the case file to the collegiate court so that it may determine the legality of the Navy’s request.

The presenting justice explained that the challenged rule does not contravene Article 129 of the Constitution because, following the reform of September 30, 2024, the functions of the Armed Forces in peacetime are no longer restricted exclusively to those directly related to military discipline.

“Article 129 of the Constitution establishes that, in peacetime, military authorities may exercise the functions provided for both in the Constitution and in the laws that emanate from it, which incorporates a principle of statutory reservation. This means that it is up to the Congress of the Union to determine, through a law in the formal and material sense, what other powers the Army, the Air Force, and the Navy of Mexico may exercise,” he said.

It should be noted that the challenged rule was repealed in November 2025, although the new law maintained the Coast Guard functions. The Court examined the merits because the contested letter was issued in 2023 and the transitional regime orders that pending matters be resolved under the previous legislation.
