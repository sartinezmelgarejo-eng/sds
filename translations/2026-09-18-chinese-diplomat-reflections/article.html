This article by Yi Fan originally appeared in the September 18, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

On the afternoon of September 15, a few hours before the Grito de Independencia, when many people were in the Zócalo awaiting the midnight bells and millions of Mexican families were celebrating the holiday at home, a post by the United States embassy in Mexico left me surprised and plunged into deep reflection. In an ironic tone, the post said that the fireworks and other symbols used in the holiday celebrations "are not made in Mexico," and it even wrote "Viva México, made in China." In the entire post, there was not a single word of congratulation for Mexico's Independence. On the contrary, it instrumentalized economic and commercial cooperation to sow discord between China and Mexico.

To those who drafted that United States embassy post, I would like to ask: on such a special day and on such an important occasion, is this post a congratulation on Mexico's Independence? Does it show the least bit of respect toward the Mexican people? Is this the conduct expected of a diplomat and the one that corresponds to diplomatic courtesy?

More than 150 years ago, President Benito Juárez said: "Among individuals, as among nations, respect for the rights of others is peace." That phrase is engraved on Mexico's monuments and also in the hearts of Mexicans and of peoples all over the world. On the other shore of the Pacific Ocean, at the beginning of its founding, the New China already proposed the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence: mutual respect for sovereignty and territorial integrity, mutual non-aggression, non-interference in the internal affairs of other countries, equality and mutual benefit, and peaceful coexistence.

Over more than seven decades, Chinese diplomacy has always followed that path based on friendship, credibility and justice. From the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence to the building of a community with a shared future for humanity, and from there to today's Four Global Initiatives —the Global Development Initiative (GDI), the Global Security Initiative (GSI), the Global Civilization Initiative (GCI) and the Global Governance Initiative (GGI)—, despite the time that has passed, China's stance has remained firm and unchanged: to respect the right of each country to independently choose its own path of development, to respect the diversity of civilizations, not to impose one's own will on others, not to force other countries to take sides and, even less, to behave like "teachers" giving orders to others.

In May 2025, at the IV Ministerial Meeting of the China-CELAC Forum, President Xi Jinping declared that China and Latin America and the Caribbean are already a community with a shared future advancing hand in hand, one that has equal treatment as its defining character, mutual benefit and shared gains as its strong driving force, openness and inclusiveness as its vision and quality, and the well-being of the peoples as its fundamental purpose.

Despite the geographic distance between China and Mexico, these two ancient millennial civilizations, located respectively in the eastern and western hemispheres, have drawn from their own histories one and the same conclusion: mutual respect is the basis for becoming friends. As a member of the embassy of China in Mexico, what we do is foster friendship, mutual understanding and greater closeness between the peoples in pursuit of common development and a better future, because we are fully aware that this is precisely what a diplomat must do.

As for the United States embassy post, the nearly unanimous criticism in the comments and the "likes" from so many internet users toward China-Mexico friendship are already the best response. What the embassy of China and its diplomats want to say is very simple: Long live Mexico! Long live the friendship between China and Mexico!

*Chinese diplomat in Mexico.
