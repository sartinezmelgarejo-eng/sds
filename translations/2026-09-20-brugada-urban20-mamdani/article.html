This article originally appeared in the September 20, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Before mayors and representatives of more than 30 cities from around the world gathered in New York, Head of Government Clara Brugada Molina called for building a global coalition for affordable housing and raised the need to promote policies that guarantee the right to remain in communities.

During her participation in the Urban 20 Forum 2026, on the panel "Housing, Affordability, and the Right to Remain," the mayor noted that for decades neoliberalism turned housing into a commodity subject to speculation, while access to a home was made conditional on families going into debt.

In Mexico City, she added, between 2018 and 2025 almost half a million people left housing poverty, but she acknowledged that problems still persist, such as housing shortages, gentrification, and the expulsion of communities driven by the speculative dynamics of the real-estate market.

Brugada recalled that in 1987 she took part as a social activist in mobilizations for the right to housing in New York, a demand that, she maintained, remains valid almost four decades later.

She explained that the capital's housing policy rests on three pillars: the massive construction of social housing, regulation of the real-estate market, and a model of co-management with communities.

She highlighted that among the goals is the construction of 200,000 housing actions by 2030, which include new housing and home improvements, with zero-interest loans granted to working families whose repayment must not exceed 20 percent of their income, and social housing that can cost up to three times less than similar properties on the market.

Brugada also reported that over the past two years 250,000 square meters of land have been acquired for social housing, and that this year 40,000 improved and transformed homes will be reached.

The strategy also includes sustainable housing, with more than 16,000 eco-technologies installed, such as rainwater-harvesting systems, solar water heaters, and urban gardens.

She announced that on the matter of rent, a law for fair, reasonable, and affordable rents is being promoted to prevent rents from rising above inflation, and she indicated that a constitutional reform initiative has been introduced to recognize the right to rootedness, create a tenant defense, and prevent families from being expelled from their neighborhoods.

As part of her activities within the Meeting of the 20 Most Influential Cities in the World, Brugada took part alongside the mayor of New York, Zohran Mamdani, in a press conference.

At that meeting, she stressed that "New York and Mexico City share a deep conviction: cities cannot become places where only those with the most can live. A truly democratic city is a city where everyone fits; where having housing, getting around, caring for a family, and enjoying public space are not privileges, but rights."

Mamdani, for his part, highlighted the mayor's work in creating and building the Utopías, as well as a public care system.

"Mexico City is a city that holds personal meaning for me, and that is also an inspiration for many of us, especially for the way it has responded to the crises of care in our economies. When we talk about care, we have talked about what your administration has done as an example of how we can offer this kind of service to people in our own city," Mamdani said during the Urban 20 Forum.

As the Head of Government put it: "It is a pleasure that this project is an inspiration in the great cities of the world."
