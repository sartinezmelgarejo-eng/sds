This article by Patricia Vázquez originally appeared in the September 20, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Cancún, Quintana Roo. A few days after the appointment of Eugenio Segura Vázquez as Coordinator of the 4T's Defense and Sovereignty Committees in Quintana Roo, the former hopeful for the post, Rafael Marín Mollinedo, warned that the arrival of "those people" does not mean they will do as they please in the state. For its part, Movimiento Ciudadano does not rule out another "Joaquinazo" —in reference to the arrival of the then-PRI member Carlos Joaquín to the governorship, backed by the opposition parties— by nominating the "wounded" who came out of the internal selection process of Morena, the PT, and the PVEM.

In a video circulating on social media, at a gathering for the birthday of his close collaborator, local deputy Ricardo Velazco, Marín Mollinedo anticipated that he is evaluating what he will do going forward, how they will move ahead, and so he called on his supporters to have patience and confidence because they will carry on.

In response, Morena founders called for expressing their displeasure through strikes and marches across the state, because they are not at all pleased that someone "from the green party, disguised in Morena maroon" wants to reach the governorship of Quintana Roo.

For his part, in an interview, Ricardo Velazco stated that the public perception is that with Eugenio Segura it will be the PVEM that governs Quintana Roo, but that the political force was built in order to recover the movement, the local governments, and the state government for the movement.

The legislator warned that Morena's National Executive Committee will have to oversee the building of inclusion agreements with the main forces that did not win, especially in the largest cities such as Cancún, Playa del Carmen, and Chetumal.

In that vein, given the national breakup of the PT with Morena and the local frictions within the 4T, the Movimiento Ciudadano council member in Cancún did not rule out that in Quintana Roo the conditions exist for a political phenomenon similar to the "Joaquinazo" of 2016 to repeat itself, and although their national leader, Dante Delgado, told them they should act with caution, he anticipated that he has met with members of the PAN and PRI to explore that possibility.

Faced with the internal conflict of other parties, the party leader stressed that Movimiento Ciudadano should not see itself as the "sole owner of the franchise," but rather maintain a receptive stance to welcome the "wounded" left behind by the selection of coordinators in the Morena-PVEM-PT coalition.
