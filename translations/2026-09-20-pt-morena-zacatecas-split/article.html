This article by Alfredo Valadez Rodríguez originally appeared in the September 20, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left-wing daily newspaper.

Zacatecas, Zac. The national leadership of the Party of Labor (PT), headed by Alberto Anaya, will decide "in the coming hours or days" whether it breaks the coalition with Morena and the PVEM in the constitutional election for the governorship of Zacatecas in 2027, over what it calls Morena's alleged "betrayal" by imposing as candidate—"winner of the polls"—Verónica Díaz Robles, who has been demonstrably linked, both by family and politically, to Monrealismo for more than 20 years, PT members consulted on the matter told this newspaper.

Verónica Díaz Robles was named candidate by the Morena-PT-PVEM coalition on September 15, and her designation caused discontent among three hopefuls from the three parties—Ulises Mejía Haro, Geovanna Bañuelos de la Torre, and Carlos Puente Salas—mainly because they consider Díaz Robles "the candidate of the governor, David Monreal Ávila," who, with his backing, has been politically and media-promoted in Zacatecas over the past two years.

She now seeks to distance herself, in Mexico City, from her kinship and closeness to the Monreal family; but in Zacatecas—where that surname carries the wear and popular disrepute of a local government in crisis—both in political circles and in society, everyone knows who is who.

In fact, it was federal deputy Ricardo Monreal Ávila who, in media statements made on Thursday, September 17, admitted that "twenty years ago" Verónica Díaz Robles married Luis Enrique Monreal Ávila (one of the 14 siblings who make up the Monreal Ávila family), with whom the aspirant had two children who bear the surname Monreal Díaz—that is, there is a blood tie, but, the coordinator of the Political Coordination Board in the Chamber of Deputies clarified, she divorced his brother 11 years ago. Paradoxically, on social media this has been refuted with the circulation of the official civil-marriage dissolution certificate, dated 2021.

Verónica Díaz, meanwhile, once anointed, launched a media campaign to distance herself from the Monreal surname, claiming that she is "Ms. Díaz." But the reality is that in recent years she has maintained her closeness and direct political collaboration with David Monreal Ávila, the current governor of Zacatecas.

For this reason, the other contenders for the candidacy within the Morena, PT and PVEM coalition—among them Senator Saúl Monreal Ávila—stated, even before the polls were conducted, that if he was excluded from the internal process under the nepotism prohibition clause, Verónica Díaz Robles should also be removed from the process, which did not happen.

In Zacatecas, meanwhile, the notion has taken hold in political circles that the PT's break with Morena could be announced officially this Monday, given that the Morena leadership "at the highest level" broke an alleged agreement with Alberto Anaya for the coalition's candidate in the state to be the PT senator Geovanna Bañuelos de la Torre—since, of the 17 governorships that will be contested in the country in 2027, the PT expressly requested "a single candidacy, that of Zacatecas," believing that here that party, with this candidate, had ample chances of winning.

Members of the PT's party leadership also stated that the party will demand that Morena show it the polls and their full methodology, on the basis of which it was decided to name Verónica Díaz Robles as candidate.

Last Wednesday, September 16, after the civic-military parade in the Zacatecas state capital, Governor David Monreal Ávila—visibly pleased with the designation of Verónica Díaz Robles, whom he publicly went so far as to call "the best option" at public events—downplayed, when expressly asked by reporters, the risk of a break with the PT and the PVEM, saying that back in 2024, in the local election for deputies and municipalities, Morena did not form an alliance with the PT and only partially with the PVEM.

David Monreal Ávila did not recall, however, that in 2010, when he first ran for governor, he was nominated by the PT, and his brother Ricardo Monreal Ávila "sacrificed" him by forcing him to withdraw mid-campaign in favor of the PRI candidate, Miguel Alonso Reyes (today one of the main allies of Alito Moreno), who on that occasion managed to defeat the leftist candidate, Antonio Mejía Haro of the PRD.

Twelve years earlier, in 1998, to become governor Ricardo Monreal Ávila resigned from the PRI because they did not make him their candidate, but he maneuvered and managed to get the PRD to nominate him, winning the election. Later, from 2004 to 2010, Amalia García Medina won the governorship for that same party of the Aztec sun (PRD).

But for the following election, Amalia García Medina put forward Antonio Mejía Haro as candidate, and Ricardo Monreal Ávila once again made the left lose when he pushed for the PRI's return to the governorship of Zacatecas, getting the tricolor party to name as its candidate the former PRD local deputy—his protégé—Miguel Alonso Reyes, who, upon winning, incorporated several former officials from Ricardo Monreal's cabinet into his administration.

Now the Monreal brothers would thus have their plan B ready: if Ms. Verónica Díaz Robles does not win—without a coalition—Miguel Alonso Reyes (who was Ricardo Monreal Ávila's private secretary when the latter was governor) is promoting former federal deputy Adolfo Bonilla Gómez to be the PRI's standard-bearer in 2027—in coalition with the PAN and PRD—so that the tricolor party could recover the governorship of Zacatecas, which Monrealismo wrested from it in 1998.
