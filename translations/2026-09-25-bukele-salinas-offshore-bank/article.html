U.S. creditors accused Grupo Salinas of having hidden strategic assets in a new subsidiary while it obtained a multimillion-dollar loan.

Mexico City, September 25 (SinEmbargo).– An offshore bank from Saint Lucia, an island country, with barely three employees, no branches serving the public, and capital of only four million dollars appears as the lender of 290 million dollars to TV Azteca, owned by Ricardo Salinas Pliego, an operation that is under scrutiny by a federal court in the United States and that involves a financial institution linked to an adviser to the government of the President of El Salvador, Nayib Bukele, an investigation by El Faro revealed.

According to the outlet, TV Azteca signed the loan with AlterBank five weeks before its commercial insolvency proceedings began in Mexico. The agreement set conditions that gave priority to the Caribbean bank over other creditors, among them The Bank of New York Mellon, trustee for bondholders to whom the broadcaster stopped paying interest on a debt of more than 630 million dollars since 2021.

The U.S. bank filed a lawsuit on June 2, 2026, before the Federal Court for the Southern District of Florida, in which it accuses TV Azteca and AlterBank of having taken part in a scheme to simulate the loan and place the offshore bank ahead of other creditors.

Indeed, last June, the creditors who are pursuing litigation in New York and who are demanding more than 600 million dollars for a debt that has not been paid accused Grupo Salinas of having hidden strategic assets in a new subsidiary, TVA III, while it obtained a 290-million-dollar loan, about which it has denied them key information.
