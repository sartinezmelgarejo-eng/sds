This article is based on reporting by José Shaddai Olvera Torres that originally appeared in the September 28, 2026 edition of El Chamuco.

Mexico City, September 28, 2026. Larry Rubin, the US Republican Party’s representative in Mexico and president of the American Society of Mexico, is pressing Mexican senators to vote down President Claudia Sheinbaum’s constitutional reform requiring the country’s top executive officeholders to hold Mexican nationality exclusively.

In a social media post addressed to all 128 senators, Rubin framed the vote as a choice between party instructions and the voters they represent. “Dual nationality does not mean dual loyalty,” he wrote.

The Chamber of Deputies approved the reform last week by 334 votes to 109, with one abstention. It now goes to the Senate, where constitutional changes require a two-thirds majority and Morena and its allies hold only a narrow margin.

The initiative amends Articles 82, 116, and 122 of the Constitution. Anyone seeking the presidency, a governorship, or the Mexico City head of government post would have to renounce any other nationality before registering as a candidate and show that the legal tie to the other country has ended. The requirement would take effect with the 2028 electoral process, leaving the 2027 midterms untouched.

Rubin has opposed the measure before: earlier in September he called it a “very serious mistake.” He argues it would divide Mexicans with one and two nationalities and hurt binational families.

Sheinbaum has defended the reform on the grounds that those who hold Mexico’s highest executive offices should have an exclusive legal and political bond with the country, saying no leader should have “two flags.”
