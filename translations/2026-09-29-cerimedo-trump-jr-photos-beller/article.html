This article by SinEmbargo Staff originally appeared in the September 29, 2026 edition of SinEmbargo, a Mexican independent digital news outlet.

Mexico City, September 29 (SinEmbargo).— Bolivian activist and lawyer Nadia Beller Delgado has once again put the spotlight on Argentine consultant Fernando Cerimedo's ties to Donald Trump's political circle. A photo she shared this Tuesday shows her ex-partner alongside Donald Trump Jr., American operative Brad Parscale, and Cerimedo's friend and consultant Andy Rivas. American political consultant Dick Morris also appears in the images — a strategist whose work in Mexico dates back to Vicente Fox's 2000 campaign and who later helped craft the fear-based "López Obrador, a danger to Mexico" messaging against Andrés Manuel López Obrador in Felipe Calderón's 2006 run.

Beller released the photo just as the U.S. government revoked the visa of Bolivia's Attorney General, Roger Mariaca, who is leading the investigation into Cerimedo over the attempted femicide against Beller herself, money laundering, and other charges — and she tied the two directly together. "What criteria is Donald Trump's government using to decide who makes this list?" she asked. "If the explanation is corruption and drug trafficking, someone needs to explain why they're pulling the visa of the Attorney General investigating a network with exactly those characteristics."

One of Cerimedo's main bridges into Trump's world has been Brad Parscale, the digital strategist who ran Trump's 2016 campaign and later led his 2020 reelection bid. After leaving Trump's inner circle, Parscale built campaign technology including Campaign Nucleus, and it was through its expansion abroad that he partnered with Cerimedo, who has publicly credited him as the "technical side" of his operation: "Brad put in all the infrastructure I work with," Cerimedo has said. Tools like Campaign Nucleus and EyesOver were used to monitor online conversations, segment audiences, and tailor messaging in campaigns Cerimedo ran across Latin America, including those of Nasry Asfura in Honduras and Rodrigo Paz in Bolivia.

Cerimedo's Trump-world ties are part of a wider right-wing network he built across Latin America. On February 10, 2026, he was named "political consultant of the year" at Mar-a-Lago, introduced by Eduardo Bolsonaro, where he spoke of the need to keep fighting the so-called "culture war" to block the return of left-wing governments.

Cerimedo was arrested a month ago in Santa Cruz de la Sierra, Bolivia, accused of the armed attack on Beller, who survived and identified him as responsible. He remains in pretrial detention on attempted-femicide charges, later joined by an illicit-enrichment case. The arrest drew attention to a web of political, business, and media ties spanning Latin America, the United States, and Mexico — where, Beller has noted, the network reaches as far as media mogul Ricardo Salinas Pliego.

In a September 21 interview on SinEmbargo Al Aire's Los Periodistas, Beller said Cerimedo built a network of political relationships that gave him access to U.S. institutions like the State Department, the CIA, and the DEA, though she rejected the idea that he was a direct operative for those agencies. Instead, she said, he used the presidents and candidates he worked for as his entry point, while selling those same politicians the idea that he had privileged contacts inside the U.S. government.

He uses the presidents to get access to institutions like the State Department, the CIA, the DEA, and so on, but at the same time, to get close to the presidents, he sells them the illusion that he's their link to Washington. So he uses them both ways, but in the end all he really has are business interests. All he cares about is making money, winning over presidents so that along the way he gets access to these countries to lobby for major companies.

Beller said Cerimedo's partners had international connections of their own, including Parscale. She claimed to have a photo of Cerimedo meeting with Parscale, Rivas, and a man she identified as "the CIA's head of intelligence," though she said she has not made that image public and does not believe it should be. She also said Parscale's technology, in her view, was used to distort public debate and wage "dirty war" in campaigns that helped presidents win, only for those same presidents to later be used as a stepping stone to the State Department.

As an example, Beller recalled a trip Cerimedo took to the United States with Rodrigo Paz, arguing it showed Cerimedo leaning on politicians for access rather than the other way around: he didn't bring Paz to Washington, she said — he went alongside him, and sat next to Paz, "not next to the State Department."
