Mexico City, September 30 (SinEmbargo).— President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo on Wednesday questioned the report published by The New York Times, which points to an alleged review of ties between Andrés Manuel López Beltrán, son of the former Mexican president, and organized crime and huachicol (fuel theft and smuggling).

Asked whether the Attorney General's Office (FGR) would have to open an inquiry on its own initiative based on the U.S. newspaper's publication, she ruled out that an investigation would be opened, considering that the publication does not present evidence.

The federal head of state responded that the content of the information should first be carefully reviewed and mentioned that the author herself establishes that there is no formal investigation into the son of former president Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO).

"Look at how the story is written, because it's worth it," President Sheinbaum commented, referring to the text released this Tuesday by The New York Times.

President Sheinbaum Pardo denied having knowledge of an investigation by the Attorney General's Office into López Beltrán and questioned the NYT over the publication. The report mentions that two U.S. security agencies are analyzing accusations about López Beltrán's alleged ties to organized crime and huachicol, although, based on sources close to the matter, the outlet itself clarifies that no formal case file has been opened.

She also maintained that the text about López Beltrán uses expressions showing that the information does not confirm the existence of an investigation: "It's not confirmed. There's no formal investigation, but it seems that maybe they told me there are some agencies that are investigating."

When pressed on whether the FGR was carrying out any investigation based on the published accusations, she said: "Well, not that I know of. The Attorney General's Office would have to report it, but we have no knowledge of it."

Two U.S. Agencies "Analyze" AMLO's Son: NYT

The New York Times report picks up accusations previously circulated by Mexican media outlets such as El Universal, Latinus, TV Azteca, and Mexicans Against Corruption and Impunity (MCCI), related to alleged business dealings, public contracts, and corporate ties of people close to López Beltrán.

Among the background cited is the relationship between former president López Obrador's sons and businessman Jorge Amílcar Olán, as well as reports on contracts related to the supply of ballast for the Tren Maya.

Although López Beltrán rejected the accusations, the U.S. newspaper also clarified that no charges have been filed against him and there is no formal investigation in the U.S.
