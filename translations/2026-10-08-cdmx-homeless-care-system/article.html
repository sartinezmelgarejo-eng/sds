This article by Darren García originally appeared in the October 8, 2026 edition of Contralínea, an investigative Mexican news outlet.

In Mexico City, the latest census presented by the capital’s government registered more than 2,800 people experiencing homelessness, exposed to health and security risks. Head of Government Clara Brugada Molina announced their incorporation into the Care System through the Network for Care, Attention and Building Autonomy for People Experiencing Homelessness. The strategy plans to set up housing spaces and comprehensive care to support them in recovering their autonomy.

People experiencing homelessness in Mexico City will be incorporated for the first time into the Care System of the capital, announced Head of Government Clara Brugada Molina. As she explained, the measure seeks to replace the traditional social assistance approach with a comprehensive public network based on rights and on attention to the vulnerabilities and capabilities of each person.

Presenting the Network for Care, Attention and Building Autonomy for People Experiencing Homelessness on October 7, 2026, the head of government noted that her administration seeks to radically transform this reality through the opening of four new spaces called Care Communities, which will replace the traditional shelter model.

The four buildings will total 16,000 square meters in central areas of the city and will have the capacity to serve more than 2,000 people. Two will be located in Perimeter A of the Historic Center and two in surrounding areas, due to the concentration of this population in the center of the capital, she explained.

The project plans to set up three Care Communities in existing buildings—one of them a government space where finance offices operated—and to build one more. Subsequently, the strategy will be extended gradually with a center in each borough, according to its needs.

The initiative is backed by the Mexico City Care System Law, approved unanimously on May 26, 2026. Its operation will be in charge of the Secretariat of Well-being and Social Equality, the Secretariat of Public Health and the System for the Integral Development of the Family of Mexico City (DIF), with the support of the agencies in charge of housing and labor.

According to data from the most recent census presented by the Secretary of Well-being and Social Equality, Pablo Enrique Yanes Rizo, the capital registers 2,878 people experiencing homelessness. They are concentrated mainly in the boroughs of Cuauhtémoc, with close to 40 percent; Gustavo A. Madero; Iztapalapa; and Venustiano Carranza. To follow up on this population, the authorities plan to update the census periodically and maintain a permanent registry.

As for children, the census counted 123 minors experiencing homelessness, 32 of them in the Cuauhtémoc borough. Their care will be in charge of the DIF, which will have a specialized shelter for abandoned children and adolescents, prioritizing their best interests and avoiding the separation of family units, according to what was announced.

The total of 2,878 people is identical to that reported by Contralínea in December 2025. In that interview, Guillermo Alan García Capcha, then executive director of the Institute for the Care of Priority Populations (IAPP), explained that the 2025 census registered an increase of 156 percent compared with 2024.

In that interview, García Capcha indicated that Cuauhtémoc concentrated 1,011 people experiencing homelessness, equivalent to about 35.1 percent of the total. He noted that the population between 18 and 59 years of age predominated and that six out of 10 people in this situation did not have an identity document.

During the October 7 presentation, Brugada Molina also announced a redistribution of institutional functions: the spaces for people with physical or intellectual disabilities, or with addiction problems, will be coordinated directly by the Secretariat of Public Health, with the aim of prioritizing medical and comprehensive care.

The Care Communities will replace the “big warehouses” with multiple beds with smaller-capacity rooms that guarantee privacy, rest and intimacy, the head of government said. She added that the buildings will have the quality, architectural design and services of the Units of Transformation and Organization for Inclusion and Social Harmony (Utopías).

Likewise, she indicated that these spaces will not function as “prison schemes”: they will have areas exclusively for families, will allow pets and will maintain flexible schedules, adapted to the work or social activities of the users. They will also include facilities for sports and for music, film, dance, theater and art activities.

The objective will be to promote the independent life of each person through access to identity, to health services with follow-up and free medicines, to nutritious food, to education and to job training. People with reduced mobility or permanent care needs will be offered physical rehabilitation and social work services, Brugada Molina emphasized.

In terms of mental health and addictions, the spaces will have specialized risk and harm reduction rooms, intensive residential care of one to six months and, progressively, a toxicology area per center. Care will be based on informed consent and on the principle of not criminalizing consumption, according to the project.

For her part, the Secretary of Public Health, Nadine Flora Gasman Zylbermann, detailed that the care pathway will have five stages: territory, assessment, Care Communities, protected homes and autonomy. She noted that the scheme seeks to guarantee that no person is discharged from a hospital, after a crisis, directly to the street and without support.

Support will be individualized through a life plan and an electronic record updated in real time, under the coordination of a multidisciplinary technical committee. The general scheme contemplates the “five A’s”: approach, attention to problems, activation of alternatives, accompaniment and building of autonomy.

The program contemplates exclusive, safe and violence-free spaces for women, with legal and psychological counseling, as well as strategies and specialized staff to serve the LGBT+ population experiencing homelessness.

Field care will be deployed through multidisciplinary territorial brigades in the 16 boroughs 24 hours a day. Made up of social work and psychology professionals, they will seek to establish bonds of trust and voluntarily channel those who accept care.

Asked by the press about the people who decide to remain on the streets, Clara Brugada said that entry into these spaces will be voluntary. In addition, she announced complementary public infrastructure, with public bathrooms, clinics and a medical unit.

In January 2027, the people served at the Coruña shelter will be transferred to the first Care Community or to the protected homes, according to their needs. To promote their economic integration, work cooperatives will be promoted within the centers and priority access to housing programs will be given to those who complete their autonomy process.

The capital’s head of government confirmed that care will also be provided to foreign migrants, who will have options to integrate economically or receive support to return to their places of origin. She anticipated that she will propose tripling, or increasing even further, the budget allocated to this care in the initiative she will send to the local Congress. She also called on civil society organizations to participate in the strategy.
