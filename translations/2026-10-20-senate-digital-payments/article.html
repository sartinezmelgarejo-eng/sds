This article by Andrea Becerril and Georgina Saldierna originally appeared in the October 20, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. Amid protests from the opposition and discontent from some Morena members, the Senate approved the new Digital Economy Law for Digital and Electronic Payments, aimed at promoting the mass use of transactions by credit card, transfers, QR codes, and other electronic means.

The bill, which was sent to the federal Executive for promulgation, does not propose an immediate and general elimination of cash payments, but it empowers the head of the Secretariat of Finance and Public Credit (SHCP) to determine, within 15 business days, the strategic sectors and relevant activities in which digital payments may become the only form of payment, under specific conditions and transition periods.

Sheffield Questions the Finance Secretariat's Powers

The presidential initiative was approved with 73 votes in favor from Morena and its allies, and 34 against, from the opposition and from Morena senator Ricardo Sheffield, who argued from the rostrum that it was “a very serious mistake” that “a technocrat who was not elected but appointed can in fact create a monopoly or price controls, fatal for the Mexican people.”

He lamented that it had not been given an in-depth review and, when PAN senator Verónica Rodríguez asked him whether he had voted against it, he replied: “Yes, I was the only idiot.”

It was one of the points the opposition debated most, even after the president of the Senate's Board of Directors, Higinio Martínez, explained that the report had been revised based on the legislators' concerns.

Morena Defends Financial Inclusion

“Digitizing a transaction does not by itself resolve economic inequalities, but having instruments that facilitate transactions, reduce paperwork, and expand access to financial services can help address them,” he stressed.

Likewise, the chair of the Economy Committee, Emmanuel Reyes, argued that the legislation seeks to expand financial inclusion, facilitate transactions, and strengthen micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs).

He explained that Mexico has infrastructure such as the Interbank Electronic Payment System (SPEI), CoDi, and Dimo, but their use still does not reach all sectors.

In presenting the report, he highlighted that during 2025 more than 7.3 billion transfers were made through SPEI. Digital payments can help small businesses record their income, expand their payment options, and generate transaction histories that make it easier to evaluate credit applications, he insisted.

Warnings About the Risks of Mandatory Digital Payments

Senators from the PAN, PRI, and MC, on the other hand, warned that the reform could impose additional costs on merchants and consumers, as well as expand the government's capacity to monitor financial transactions.

PAN senator José Máximo García questioned that the Finance Secretariat could determine the sectors in which digital payment will be mandatory without, in his view, sufficiently clear criteria. He warned that connectivity problems and lack of access to banking services persist in rural communities, a concern shared by Néstor Camarillo of MC.

Along the same lines, PRI senator Carolina Viggiano stressed that the necessary infrastructure does not exist and insisted that “We cannot give this power to the Finance Secretariat. You know the Finance Secretariat, they don't even receive you, they don't even accept that you change a comma, they are authoritarian and the only thing they want is to collect money.”

Her fellow party member Claudia Anaya said the new law contradicts the Monetary Law and insisted that the transition period for moving exclusively to digital payments is not concretely established.

According to the transitional provisions, once the decree takes effect, the Finance Secretariat will have 15 business days to determine the sectors and activities in which digital payments may be made exclusive.

Subsequently, the competent authorities will have another 15 business days, from the publication of that determination, to issue the corresponding provisions and establish the transition mechanisms.

The proposal also provides that, when a technical or connectivity contingency makes it impossible to receive electronic payments, the use of cash or checks will be temporarily allowed.

In addition, it links digital payments with e-government tools such as the Digital CURP, the Citizen Digital File, and the Commercial Establishments Platform, with the aim of reducing time, travel, and bureaucratic burdens for businesses, the report highlights.
