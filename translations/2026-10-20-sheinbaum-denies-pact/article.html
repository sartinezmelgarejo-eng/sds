(OCTOBER 20, 2026).-The President rejected claims that the arrest of Chihuahua's former prosecutor, César Jáuregui, is part of a political agreement in exchange for offering an ambassadorship to the PAN governor.

President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo denied having exonerated the governor of Chihuahua, María Eugenia Campos Galván, known as Maru Campos, over the alleged involvement of agents of the United States Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) in a security operation carried out on Mexican territory without federal authorization.

During her morning press conference this Tuesday, October 20, Sheinbaum rejected reports of an alleged political agreement with the state governor to concentrate judicial responsibility on Chihuahua's former attorney general, César Jáuregui Moreno, in exchange for offering her an ambassadorship.

The statement comes amid investigations into the participation of U.S. agents in an operation against a clandestine laboratory in the community of El Pinal, in the municipality of Morelos, Chihuahua, last April. The case led to judicial proceedings against Jáuregui for alleged irregularities related to the intervention of foreign personnel.

Sheinbaum maintained that her government does not negotiate criminal responsibility or intervene to decide who should be investigated. She also rejected the idea that the former prosecutor's arrest was the result of a negotiation between the federal and state governments.

Sheinbaum: “It Is Not My Role” to Exonerate Maru Campos

The President clarified that her previous statements about the governor of Chihuahua do not constitute an exoneration. On Monday she had said that, with the information known up to that point, there was no evidence showing that Campos had directly ordered the operation.

This Tuesday she insisted that determining responsibility is a matter for the prosecuting authorities.

“It is not my role. In any case, it is the role of the prosecutor to investigate, and the Prosecutor's Office investigates as far as there is evidence,” she explained.

The President maintained that it is up to the Federal Attorney General's Office (FGR) to establish, based on the evidence, who was responsible for the events. Therefore, the lack of known evidence directly linking the governor to the order for the operation does not amount to a definitive ruling on her possible knowledge or participation.

The Case of the CIA Agents in Chihuahua

The investigation focuses on the alleged participation of U.S. agents in security activities in Chihuahua without the required federal authorization. The April operation in El Pinal ended with the discovery of a clandestine laboratory; afterward, a vehicle accident caused the deaths of two U.S. agents and two Mexican officials.

The state Prosecutor's Office pointed to Jáuregui for alleged irregularities related to the participation of foreign personnel in activities of the State Investigation Agency. Separately, the FGR accused him of treason in connection with the case.

The investigations leave open questions about the authorization of those activities, what various authorities may have known, and the chain of responsibility. The legal situation of each person must be determined in the corresponding proceedings.

Sheinbaum reiterated that the intervention of foreign agents in field operations without authorization constitutes a matter of national security and argued that the case should be investigated in accordance with the law.
