This article by Lorenzo Meyer originally appeared in the October 22, 2026 edition of SinEmbargo, an independent Mexican digital news outlet.

Hernán Cortés traveled far and wide across what is today Mexico, but he never made it to what is today Washington. However, recently Cortés and other similar spirits entered the U.S. State Department, summoned by its current Secretary, Marco Rubio, to accompany him on a trip to Europe. Once on the old continent, Rubio invoked those great ghosts of the distant past and presented them in a speech he delivered on the hill of the Pnyx in Athens, a historic site where in classical antiquity the Ekklesía, or popular assembly, of that city met, and which has nothing less than the Parthenon as its backdrop. And the context in which, for a moment, the conqueror -and destroyer- of ancient Mexico was conjured up as a member of a group of great invaders and conquerors of the Western world is revealing of the spirit that Donald Trump's dynamic collaborator seeks to reactivate both in his country and among the allies and sympathizers of the United States in Europe. But, above all, it is revealing of the image that Trumpism would like the world to have of what that Make America Great Again is supposed to imply, and which implies dignifying Trumpism to the point of making it the current heir to the essence of the so-called “Western Civilization.” It is an image full of positive energy but one that omits all the negative and brutal features that have accompanied that “civilization.” In short, the Secretary of State's idea of placing Cortés and ultimately Trump as bearers of the essence of Western civilization leads to deepening doubts about the virtues of said civilization and about Trumpism as its reincarnation.

Let us look in a little more detail at what the Secretary said on the subject and, above all, the circumstances in which he said it.

For Marco Rubio there is no doubt that the United States is the legitimate heir to the worldview that was forged and developed in what we know as classical Greece, that is, the one that existed in the 5th and 4th centuries B.C., and which today we remember positively above all when reading Plato and Aristotle, when admiring the beauty of the Parthenon, or when recalling the heroism of King Leonidas and the handful of Spartans who faced the Persian invaders to the end at the pass of Thermopylae. In politics, from that Greece of philosophers and sages we celebrate the idea of democracy as a system of government and of the exercise of authority based not on divine designs but on the will of the citizen majority and on the use of reason and empirical knowledge to explain the world. However, we must also take into account the slave-based foundation of its economy, its discrimination against foreigners, and the inferior status of women.

Before his elite European audience, Secretary Marco Rubio extolled the spirit of the West in matters of conquest and the imposition of the will of its great warrior leaders on other societies, and highlighted the chain of successful conquerors that runs from Alexander the Great to Julius Caesar to Charlemagne, passing through Hernán Cortés, to continue with the vigor of the great empire created in the 19th and 20th centuries by that small island that is England, which came to cover almost a fifth of the globe, but he conveniently omitted the issue of the plunder, exploitation, and even genocide of the conquered societies, to finally culminate in what Rubio considers today to be the end of that chain of giants -who were giants as much for their capacity for action and their daring as for their cultivation of science and their Christian values-: the United States. Personally, the Secretary of State appeared particularly proud before the European representatives of his own European-Spanish lineage.

Marco Rubio insisted in the speech in question that the United States intends to take to new heights what for him constitutes an unparalleled history of 2,500 years of Western excellence. In that context, the Secretary urged the leaders and peoples of Western Europe to support and follow a United States full of confidence in its ability to keep moving forward in the supposedly great and unequaled enterprise of keeping Western Civilization as a whole great again.

Well then, how should we interpret this speech in Athens by President Trump's foreign policy spokesman? On the one hand, it can be seen as an attempt to exorcise the idea of China and of the East in general as the emerging and successful rivals of the United States and its European allies in the competitive and harsh world of competition among the great powers. There is no doubt that the United States remains the leading military power, but there is also no doubt that it has already ceased to be the undisputed center it was of the unipolar international system after the disappearance of the Soviet Union in 1991, where Washington's priorities were effectively imposed. On the other hand, today none of the countries that make up that so-called “Western World” has the slightest possibility of coming to occupy a position of leadership that rivals the one the United States came to have, and it is increasingly clear that it is China in particular and other countries of the East, such as India, that have an economic, political, and scientific dynamism that rivals that of the United States, not to mention their demographic dynamism. It is possible, then, that Marco Rubio's speech was written less as a call to a Europe from which, in practice, President Trump is increasingly distancing himself (everything indicates, for example, that in the case of the NATO-Russia war over Ukraine, Trump no longer decisively supports his European allies) and that in reality that speech was an effort to exorcise the possibility that the far eastern end of Eurasia will be the new center of the international system of the 21st century.

According to the calculations of a great expert in world economic history, Professor Jeffrey Sachs of the Center for Sustainable Development at Columbia University, for almost two millennia China has been a great power, and although it has experienced some periods of decline, it has always recovered, and now it is doing so again spectacularly. Today, and in the eyes of all, China is living through one of those moments of great recovery while the United States is undergoing a process of deindustrialization and excessive indebtedness that already exceeds its own GDP, and whose governing elite shows signs of serious failures in its capacity to govern. None of the members of Trump's cabinet has excellent preparation in the technical or political areas they are responsible for administering. By contrast, according to a query to ChatGPT and after examining the résumés of the Chinese leaders, it turns out that the top Chinese leader, Xi Jinping, has a doctorate in law from Tsinghua University and heads a government that is: “[A] political elite with extensive graduate training and [with] significant technocratic components. [In that circle] academic credentials coexist with long careers within the [Communist] Party.” In Washington's current government, the situation is almost the opposite.

In conclusion, the Greek heritage of the West, which for a time was almost lost, was reborn -curiously with the help of the great Arab intellectuals-; it is undoubtedly a great heritage, but in practice it is mixed with and founded on brutal violence, exploitation, and inhumanity toward its own societies and, above all, toward the civilizations it colonized, as is the case of Mexico. That is the dark part of its heritage and it is also, therefore, a central part of the history of the United States as the great power it has been and seeks to continue being, but which today is already running into the rebirth of the Eastern civilizations, and its dream of maintaining its world hegemony is already an impossible dream.
