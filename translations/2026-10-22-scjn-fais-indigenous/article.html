This article by Darren García originally appeared in the October 21, 2026 edition of Contralínea, a Mexican investigative journalism magazine.

Unanimously, the plenary of the Supreme Court of Justice of the Nation (SCJN) upheld that municipalities must deliver directly at least 10 percent of the resources of the Social Infrastructure Contribution Fund (FAIS) to Indigenous and Afro-Mexican peoples and communities, in compliance with the constitutional provisions that recognize their autonomy.

During the session, the justices rejected the arguments of 12 constitutional controversies that demanded the delivery of the resources to municipal authorities, considering that the measure does not violate their fiscal autonomy. Likewise, they recognized the validity of the provisions of the 2026 Federal Expenditure Budget that empower the Secretariat of Well-Being to issue the fund's guidelines. Regarding the rules corresponding to 2025, they dismissed the challenges, because those rules were no longer in effect.

The controversies were filed by the Cuauhtémoc borough of Mexico City and the municipalities of San Nicolás de los Garza, Nuevo León; Huixquilucan and Metepec, State of Mexico; San Andrés Cholula, Puebla; Kanasín, Yucatán; Irapuato, Guanajuato; La Piedad, Michoacán; San Juan de los Lagos and Chapala, Jalisco; San Juan del Río, Querétaro; and San Luis Potosí, in the state of the same name.

The plaintiffs alleged a violation of their fiscal autonomy and argued that the Secretariat of Well-Being acted as an intermediate authority in the administration of the fund. However, the plenary rejected those arguments, since FAIS federal contributions have specific purposes and are not subject to the regime of free administration of municipal resources.

The Court concluded that this provision responds to the mandate of Article 2 of the Constitution, whose September 2024 reform recognizes peoples and communities as subjects of public law, with legal personality and their own assets, as well as the capacity to receive and directly administer the budget allocations that correspond to them. The aim is to strengthen their autonomy and help reduce inequality gaps.

For fiscal year 2026, the Indigenous and Afro-Mexican component of the FAIS, called FAISPIAM, has 13.506 billion pesos, equivalent to 10 percent of the fund's resources. This budget is intended to finance basic social infrastructure works, such as drinking water, drainage, electrification, housing improvement, roads, and community services.

The draft resolutions were prepared by Justice Giovanni Azael Figueroa Mejía, who maintained that these measures represent “an important step forward in making effective rights that have historically been postponed and guaranteeing genuine equality.”

For his part, the Chief Justice of the SCJN, Hugo Aguilar Ortiz, considered that the resolution closes a dark chapter for Indigenous and Afro-Mexican peoples and communities, by strengthening the recognition of their autonomy. He recalled that, for a long time, their rights were limited, even in the agrarian sphere, where they were recognized only as de facto communities.
