Mexico City, October 26 (SinEmbargo).– The former director of Strategic Operations of the Chihuahua Prosecutor's Office, Guillermo Arturo Zuany Portillo, is a fugitive. He took with him the keys to closed doors holding secrets about the operations of the Central Intelligence Agency, the CIA, on Mexican soil. He knows what led the foreign agents to take part in an operation on April 17 and 18, in the community of El Pinal, in the municipality of Morelos. Did they go to a drug lab or did they go for something else? Because neither the CIA nor the state Prosecutor's Office has the authority to pursue drugs or drug traffickers.

The story of Zuany Portillo is that of someone who dances to the tune the United States plays. A tune that, it seems, the Governor of Chihuahua, María Eugenia Campos Galván, also dances to. Zuany was promoted on September 5, 2025 to the Specialized Prosecutor's Office for Strategic Operations from the State Investigation Agency. And three months later, as the Governor herself has recounted, the United States withdrew his visa, a mechanism that the State Department of the United States uses to pressure politicians and blackmail governments around the world.

For unknown reasons, Washington gave Zuany Portillo his visa back and for even more inexplicable reasons, Campos Galván reinstated him in the post that allowed the police chief to bring the foreign agents into Mexico. Because it is precisely with his return to César Jáuregui's Prosecutor's Office that the Americans began operating in national territory. The fugitive is, very probably, the Cerberus guarding secrets that lead to the ousted former Chihuahua prosecutor and the Governor.

Zuany Allegedly Facilitated Entry of Agents to Chihuahua

The details of Zuany Portillo's actions and of the alleged foreign interference in Mexican territory were recorded in the investigation file that led to the arrest of former state prosecutor César Jáuregui Moreno, who was charged at the federal level with the crime of Treason Against the Homeland by the Attorney General's Office (FGR), a charge for which he is already detained and held in the Altiplano maximum security prison.

According to sources close to the case file who spoke to the Spanish newspaper El País, Zuany operated as the main facilitator for the US agents to intervene in the operational and institutional activities of the Chihuahua Prosecutor's Office, for at least six months before the incursion in El Pinal culminated in the deaths of two CIA agents and two local officials, among them Pedro Ramón Oseguera Cervantes, who had been appointed head of the State Investigation Agency (AEI) six months earlier, which coincided with Zuany Portillo's reinstatement.

Héctor Villasana, defense attorney for former prosecutor Jáuregui Moreno, highlighted that it was the Governor of Chihuahua herself, together with Santiago De la Peña, secretary general of Government, who reinstated Zuany after the United States Government withdrew his visa. "They took away his visa and even so they rehired Zuany," the litigator said on Thursday during the hearing to bind the former prosecutor over to trial for the crime of illegal exercise of public service, statements reported by the local newspaper Tiempo.

As part of the reshuffle in the leadership team of the FGE, which coincided with Zuany's rehiring, Pedro Ramón Oseguera Cervantes took over as head of the AEI; however, months later he lost his life in the road accident after the anti-narcotics operation in El Pinal, carried out between April 17 and 18 in that community, located in the municipality of Morelos.

The Operation That Unleashed a Political Crisis

The first time the El Pinal operation became known was on April 18, when the Chihuahua FGE presented it as one of the most significant blows to drug trafficking in the country, in which two methamphetamine labs were found in the sierra, between Morelos and Guachochi. In the operation, in which there were no arrests, the AEI and the Secretariat of National Defense took part, the latter notified only to provide perimeter support once the forces were already deployed in the area.

However, during the official announcement Prosecutor Jáuregui omitted mentioning the participation of the CIA agents or the road accident in which two Mexican agents and the Americans Richard Leiter Johnston III, 35 years old, and John Dudley Black, 43 years old, lost their lives.

It was not until the next day that the United States Ambassador to Mexico, Ronald Johnson, released a message of condolences for the deaths of two members of the Embassy staff, as well as two Chihuahua officials. Later, then-Prosecutor César Jáuregui confirmed that the Americans were instructors from the diplomatic mission. Meanwhile, it is now known, Arturo Zuany gave instructions to recover the foreign agents' belongings.

In addition to the agents, who it was later learned belonged to the CIA, Pedro Román Oseguera Cervantes, director of the AEI, and Manuel Genaro Méndez Montes, a member of his security team, died. The vehicle in which they were traveling fell into a ravine of approximately 180 meters and exploded.

The accident unleashed a serious political crisis between the federal Government and the Government of Chihuahua when President Claudia Sheinbaum publicly reproached Governor Maru Campos for allowing the unauthorized intervention of foreign intelligence agencies and carrying out federal-type operations without notifying the National Security Cabinet. The confrontation led to mutual accusations between the Morena and National Action (PAN) parties. The former accused Treason Against the Homeland and the latter pointed to alleged collusion with organized crime.

Faced with political pressure, Campos met with Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch and established the Specialized Investigation Unit, which triggered two days later, on April 27, the resignation of César Jáuregui, who acknowledged that there had been omissions in the information and in the institutional management related to the Americans.

However, Arturo Zuany remained in his post two more weeks until submitting his resignation on May 13. When the state investigation unit pointed to him as being related to the United States agents, which changed the initial version that the one responsible was only the head of the AEI, Zuany fled.

But the pressure did not let up, so on Tuesday, May 12, the head of the FGE Specialized Unit, Wendy Paola Chávez Villanueva, gave a press conference in which she confirmed the presence of the CIA agents in the facilities of the Strategic Operations Prosecutor's Office on at least two prior dates and hours before the convoy left for the operation in the municipality of Morelos, on April 16. One of them even had a firearm.

“The investigative acts carried out allow us to notice elements that suggest the possible intervention, knowledge or participation of other FGE officials related to the operation under investigation,” Chávez said, and then announced the resignation of Zuany Portillo resulting from “an administrative procedure for a possible omission.”

Although since then Zuany Portillo was pointed to as one of those responsible for the presence of the CIA agents in Chihuahua, it was not until five months later, in mid-October, that a Judge issued formal arrest warrants against Jáuregui and Zuany for the crimes of illegal exercise of public service and false statements. Nevertheless, the former director of the Specialized Prosecutor's Office for Strategic Operations has not yet been formally charged by the FGR with the crime of Treason Against the Homeland.

However, with Oseguera dead and Zuany a fugitive, the investigation maintains key gaps about those directly responsible who allowed, and coordinated, the entry and participation of US agents in Mexican territory.

Zuany Was Appointed by Maru Campos

Last night, in an interview on the program "Los Periodistas," hosted by Álvaro Delgado Gómez and Alejandro Páez Varela, Luis Villegas Montes, attorney for César Jáuregui, recalled that Arturo Zuany Portillo, who was serving as specialized Prosecutor for Strategic Operations and Anti-Kidnapping when the El Pinal operation occurred, and who is a fugitive from justice, was appointed to the post by María Eugenia Campos herself and Santiago de la Peña, current Secretary General of Government of the State of Chihuahua.

“We have to remember that the one who appoints Zuany is the Governor [Maru Campos] and the Secretary General of Government; the official letter with which Zuany starts working is precisely at the proposal of the Governor and Santiago de la Peña, that brings us to this point of how much the Governor knew or not,” he said in the conversation broadcast on the SinEmbargo Al Aire channel on the YouTube platform.

The attorney asked not to fall into speculation and to focus on the facts. He explained that for César Jáuregui to be declared guilty for the “CIA Gate,” it must first be proven that the presence of foreign agents in Chihuahua was due to an express order from the former prosecutor.

“It makes me a little afraid to launch ourselves into speculation because one starts to stray from the issue of what should matter to us in the defense. Our position is that they must prove the presence of the foreign agents in the field and must prove that the presence obeys expressly an order issued or given by the Prosecutor. As long as it does not happen that way the Prosecutor is protected by the principle of presumption of innocence, everything else only serves to illustrate a political, not legal, framework,” he said.

Luis Villegas concluded that after the interviews and statements made by Governor Maru Campos this day it is evident that the state governor had knowledge of the presence of the CIA agents and that Zuany was her agent and not César Jáuregui's, as she has tried to make it seem.

“From these conversations, from María Eugenia's interviews, from this matter of the case file, I conclude that there was coordination, that the investigation begins at the request of the government of the state of Chihuahua, that the head of the Executive in Chihuahua is María Eugenia Campos and therefore the one responsible, and that Zuany was more an agent of María Eugenia than of Jáuregui,” affirmed the attorney, who is also a PAN member in the state.

Maru Accused Jáuregui Over Operation and Corruption

After Maru Campos gave an interview on October 22 to news presenter Carlos Loret de Mola, in which she offered details about the “tense” meeting she held with the President on September 19 over the El Pinal operation, Sheinbaum Pardo revealed that it was the Governor who accused her Prosecutor, César Gustavo Jáuregui Moreno, of being the one who allowed CIA agents into Chihuahua, and accused him of being corrupt.

The President recounted this, and added that Campos denounced the corruption of her party colleague.

"She told me that the former prosecutor has casinos, that that is what they had found when she moved the Prosecutor. What did the President tell her? 'Proceed, as any citizen should proceed when they find acts that violate the Law,'" Sheinbaum added.

In light of the complaint made by the Governor of Chihuahua against her former prosecutor, President Sheinbaum recommended that the state Prosecutor's Office initiate the corresponding procedure to investigate this case and offered to put her in contact with the head of the FGR to report these facts.

"I told her, 'why doesn't the Justice Prosecutor's Office of the State of Chihuahua proceed, and if you like I'll mention it to the Attorney General of the Republic so that she receives you,'" the Chief Executive recounted.

In addition, the Chief Executive confirmed what Campos said, who highlighted that “there was a very strong, very tense conversation, a conversation full of reproaches, of complaints, mutual, they were complaints, mutual reproaches, above all about the security situation in the state of Chihuahua and about the security situation in the country.”

"Indeed we saw each other in a small room at the Ciudad Juárez airport. She started the conversation reproaching me. We raised our voices at some point, yes, both of us," she admitted.

"She reproached me for why I had accused her of Treason Against the Homeland. I told her 'no, it has to be investigated why foreign agents entered without permission and in accordance with the National Security Law," the Chief Executive detailed. "I told her 'I didn't accuse you, I said that the Government of Chihuahua or someone had violated the National Security Law. And you said that we were the Government of death, well, what is that?', I told her," she added.

For her part, the state governor detailed that the meeting took place at the Ciudad Juárez airport, Chihuahua, and that the federal Chief Executive was very angry about the US interference that Campos had allegedly authorized, so much so that, the Governor asserted, they raised their voices and made mutual reproaches. “Very tense, very tense, she was very angry and I was also very angry, because yes we raised our voices, we both raised our voices, because she had a grievance over me having brought the American agents into Mexico, supposedly,” Campos said.

“‘You brought the Americans in through the back door and you generated a conflict with the United States, and you are the only Governor with whom I cannot have a good relationship, you are the only Governor who kind of doesn’t let herself be pushed around, and I have a very strong grievance for three things: the American agents in the drug lab; the grievance because you called me the President of death’ [...] and a third, that there were American agents inside the Torre Centinela.”

Nevertheless, the Governor insisted on collaboration with the United States: “There has been very institutional collaboration on the part of the Secretariat of Public Security with the government of the United States, that is why what happened now in the state Prosecutor's Office kept us so tense, because within the relationship with the United States there was a lot of coordination and a lot of collaboration day to day, because a border state, with the longest border in the country, has to have that relationship day to day,” she justified.

In addition, Campos maintained that in recent weeks she has been harshly attacked by the so-called Fourth Transformation, so she lashed out at Ariana Montiel, national leader of Morena, whom she accused of being related to organized crime. “This week has been unbearable, the attacks that the 4T itself, Morena itself, Ariana Montiel herself have made against me, who has a lot to explain and a lot to say about organized crime and these terrorist groups in the state of Chihuahua,” she said.
