This article by Darren García originally appeared in the October 27, 2026 edition of Revista Contralínea, Mexico's leading investigative journalism magazine.

The disappearance of persons in Mexico City fell 30 percent between 2024 and 2026, while the number of people located each week rose 68 percent, said the head of government, Clara Brugada Molina.

Presenting the results and the search strategy, the capital's security cabinet reported that 98 percent of cases conclude with the person being located alive, and 50 percent of people are found within the first 24 hours, due to the guidelines of the new protocols of the Specialized Group for Immediate Search Response. This strategy made it possible to locate 1,204 people whose reports corresponded to years prior to 2025, the authorities added.

According to the report, presented at the head of government's press conference, the vast majority of disappearances in the capital are not related to crime, since 86 percent of the reports correspond to voluntary absences due to health problems, family conflicts or personal decisions, and only 1 percent of the people were victims of serious crimes. The authorities clarified that in Mexico City there are no records of enforced disappearance committed by the State.

Celebrating these results, Brugada Molina noted that they are explained, in part, by an increase in the amount allocated to these areas: 460 million pesos, of which 41 million went to the Comprehensive Care Center for the Search for Persons, which has already opened and has 300 officials operating 24 hours a day, as well as 142 million pesos to build the Temporary Custody Center. Likewise, more than 200 million pesos will be used to hire some 150 new specialized agents.

The head of government recalled the 30 commitments designed together with search collectives and asserted that progress has been made on 83 percent of them. In addition, she signed 21 new ones, for a total of 51 to be addressed as a priority.

Among these new commitments are: incorporating 100 additional investigative police officers and 50 agents for the public prosecutor's offices, which will be possible with an investment of 200 million pesos, she detailed.

Also contemplated are a Genetic Identification Plan that totals 10,000 profiles, financial support of 2,500 pesos per month for affected children and the development of a metropolitan-level search strategy, in coordination with the State of Mexico and Morelos, scheduled for the first days of November.

Brugada Molina asserted that her administration avoids the revictimization of family members and noted that, even if years have passed since a person's disappearance, the search for them will continue. She added: "no absence should become oblivion while there is a single family waiting."
