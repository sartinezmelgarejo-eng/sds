This article by Anahí Del Ángel originally appeared in the October 27, 2026 edition of Revista Contralínea, Mexico's leading investigative journalism magazine.

The Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS) will launch a plan of 12 actions that must be completed in 120 days, from October 27 to February 24, with an investment of 11.745 billion pesos, to improve medical care, reduce the surgical backlog, guarantee the supply of medicines and supplies and strengthen emergency services, reported its director general, Zoé Robledo Aburto.

The program includes a salary increase of 8 percent for 504,000 active workers, the conversion of 10,939 employees to permanent positions, the hiring of 2,500 medical specialists and the acquisition of medical equipment, beds, stretchers and wheelchairs, in addition to actions to avoid the cancellation of specialty consultations at the close of the year and to speed up the filling of prescriptions.

Robledo Aburto explained that the measures arose from the needs expressed by the institute's workers during tours of the hospitals, in which they gathered proposals on working conditions, the availability of equipment and the supplies required to care for beneficiaries.

Regarding the salary increase, he detailed that, on the instruction of President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo, dialogue with the union was resumed and efficiencies in spending were sought in order to raise from 6.8 to 8 percent the increase that had been proposed and approved in Congress. The adjustment will be distributed as 4 percent to the base salary and another 4 percent to the rent assistance.

The IMSS director specified that the 6.8 percent increase already represented a budgetary effort of 20 billion pesos, while raising it to 8 percent will involve 5.229 billion additional pesos. The increase began to be reflected this October 27 and will also be seen in the following day's payments.

As an example, he noted that a medical specialist with one year of seniority and a full-time schedule, who earned 36,896 pesos net per month, will receive 39,120 pesos, that is, 2,224 pesos more.

In the case of a general nurse with the same seniority, income will go from 19,607 to 20,792 pesos per month, not counting the additional benefits contemplated in the collective bargaining agreement.

Regarding the conversion to permanent positions, Robledo reported that 10,939 workers with temporary contracts, known as "08", will obtain a permanent position during the next 120 days. Of that total, 7,378 correspond to workers who joined the institute during the covid-19 pandemic and who had not yet been incorporated into a permanent position.

Among the categories considered are 6,363 nurses, 1,439 doctors and 673 stretcher-bearers, in addition to hygiene and cleaning, warehouse, service window and maintenance staff. The measure represents an additional investment of 3.14 billion pesos.

To this will be added the hiring of 2,500 medical specialists, whose call will be published on November 3, with emphasis on hard-to-fill positions, particularly in internal medicine, emergency medicine and radiology. The objective is to fill positions for which there are not enough candidates, especially in places where it is difficult to guarantee care on all shifts.

The institute will also incorporate 100 pieces of high-technology medical equipment, among them three linear accelerators, four 3-tesla magnetic resonance machines, 26 256-slice CT scanners and 67 mammography machines. According to the head of the IMSS, this is state-of-the-art equipment that will add to the previously announced acquisitions.

In addition, 2,917 pieces of equipment for medical services will be purchased, among them anesthesia machines, C-arms for surgical procedures and mobile vital signs monitors, known as "robotinas," used by nursing staff to measure patients' blood pressure, oxygenation and temperature. Corrective and preventive maintenance will also be given to some 4,756 pieces of equipment that require repairs or accessories.

The official noted that the investment of 11.745 billion pesos will be financed, among other measures, through new contracting schemes provided for in the Acquisitions Law and direct purchases from manufacturers.

As an example, he indicated that the direct acquisition of anesthesia equipment will save about 400 million pesos compared to the cost of contracting a comprehensive service.
