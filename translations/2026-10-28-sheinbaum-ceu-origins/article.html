FIRST OF THREE PARTS: 1986. The "Plebeian Rebellion" Is Born at UNAM. And It Grows. And Then It Takes Power ¬ Part 1

SECOND PART OF THREE: How the 1968 Rebellion Influenced That of 1986. This Is How the CEU Was Built ¬ Part 2

Mexico City, October 28 (SinEmbargo).– The tensest episode of the dialogue between the University Student Council (CEU) and the UNAM Rectorate before the strike four decades ago was on January 16, 1987, when porros (hired thugs) from "Voz Universitaria," who obeyed the authorities, occupied the Che Guevara auditorium of the Faculty of Philosophy and Letters from early on, seeking to violently blow up the negotiation, and then a young woman made herself heard by the crowd: Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo.

"First of all, compañeros, absolute silence and respect; second, let us not fall for provocations; third, I ask the CEU compañeros who are in this wing to open a path for the moment when the compañeros of the commission have to pass, that they show a lot of respect," Sheinbaum instructed her fellow members of the movement, and immediately addressed the "porros" who, in front of her and the UNAM authorities, were holding up signs.

"We ask the compañeros who come here, the compañeros from Law who are carrying these signs, to maintain the same respect that we have maintained toward you. The responsibility of the CEU shows, compañeros. Any provocation will be your fault, compañeros, and we ask that you respect the compañeros of the commission."

How did she go from student leader at UNAM's Faculty of Sciences, and before that at the Colegio de Ciencias y Humanidades (CCH) Sur, to arriving at the National Palace as the first woman President of Mexico and the first scientist to reach that office? Part of this story is told by her compañeros from the CEU, of which she was a representative as a Physics student.

From the Student Movement to the Presidency

Guadalupe "La Pita" Carrasco Licea, who was the representative of the Faculty of Sciences and is currently a teacher at CCH Sur, speaks of Sheinbaum's arrival at the Presidency of Mexico, as successor to Andrés Manuel López Obrador, as a triumph of women and of society.

"I believe that both López Obrador's arrival and Claudia's have a very long history behind them that includes, of course, the CEU movement and other movements that took place throughout the country in different places and so on, I mean, I think López Obrador was quite aware of that, he made it explicit frequently, I mean: 'I know I am not here because of my pretty face but because of everything that many people fought for,' etcetera, etcetera.

"I don't feel it is a generational issue: Well, here at the CCH you can't imagine how happy the teachers were because, besides, Claudia studied here at Sur before going to the Faculty of Sciences, and all those who were her teachers started making comments, 'she was my student in such and such subject' and 'excellent student,' etcetera, etcetera, etcetera, right? I feel it is not so much a generational issue, I think it is something important to show that the presence of women in national political life can reach the Presidency, something that seemed forbidden, that it seemed we would never achieve, well, even thinking of a woman Rector of UNAM was frankly difficult and it still has its problems, eh!, a woman Rector of UNAM is not so easy to happen.

"So that, of course, is a triumph of society and of the struggle of women, of the struggle of women for many, many years in very different contexts, both of intellectuals and of popular struggle, of the women who fought for tortillas, for the price of tortillas, for gas, for having decent conditions in their neighborhoods, etcetera, it is a triumph of all those sectors, but, let's say that all those sectors are not equally represented in those who embody the Presidency."

Andrea González was one of the youngest CEU representatives in the negotiations with the UNAM Rectorate. She was 16 years old when she represented Preparatoria 4 in Tacubaya, and she never thought she would see Sheinbaum as President of Mexico.

"I didn't imagine it but it seems very logical to me, honestly, that is, not only because of the formative force of discussion that the University had in those years, but also because of the political capacity that Claudia Sheinbaum always showed

"Did you know her?"

"Yes, yes, yes I knew her and the truth is that, I mean, she was one of the best-prepared people, the most generous, I mean with me, from the student group perhaps one of the most generous. So yes, it seems to me that she is a person with a very remarkable moral integrity and political capacity."

Layla Méndez, 16 years old and representative of Preparatoria 5 in Coapa, was Sheinbaum's compañera in the historic current, and has maintained a relationship with her since then.

"I knew, of course, the current President, of course. I can tell you that she is a person who, if you ask me, is she studious?, she is extremely dedicated, she always was, I say she is a very good friend of mine in the sense of camaraderie, she was, in a way, in Sciences, an engine of confrontation also with another leader who was Guadalupe Carrasco, but the positions were always debated, there was never a matter of fistfights, of violence, not even verbal, not even verbal, so I can say that the entire trajectory she had at the University, she did it on the basis of one single thing, personal effort.

Imanol Ordorika, one of the historic leaders of the CEU, is a witness to Sheinbaum's evolution, from when they were compañeros in the National Front Against Repression and the Eureka Committee of Rosario Ibarra de Piedra until they were active together in the Party of the Democratic Revolution (PRD), and he saw her win the highest public office in the country.

"I met Claudia Sheinbaum when she was, I think, 16 years old and was a student activist at the Colegio de Ciencias y Humanidades with Mireya Imaz, and we were active together in the magazine Punto Crítico and since then, well, we were friends, we continued, I consider that even though we haven't seen each other in a long time, we are friends, we have very strong shared experiences. For example, in a recent article I recalled how in 1978 we were supporting the hunger strike of the mothers of the disappeared at the Metropolitan Cathedral and the presence of Claudia and Mireya in those actions.

"So, they also arrived at the CEU with quite an intense experience and well yes, what you say, it was unforeseeable that she would be, 40 years later, the President of Mexico, and that is mediated by a very long trajectory in different areas, which is what has happened to all of us in that generation of activists who were formed, although we had previous experience, but this was a foundational formative act, a historic reference point for all of us, we moved on marked by that experience, along different paths in life, in academia, in party politics, in social movement struggles, in mass media, and in particular Claudia went through several because she was in academia and later, much later, in party politics."

From the CEU to the Ballot Box

Carlos Ímaz, another of the historic leaders of the CEU, was Sheinbaum's compañero in struggle and in life for many years. When he speaks of her as President of Mexico, he does so with personal and generational pride, because it was a long political process of struggle that they shared.

"Well, for us it is a source of pride, let's say, and it seems to me a great generational victory. I mean, because what you say is very true. We fought first defending the university and public education. And we discussed this a lot internally and in assemblies, don't think it was in closed groups. That was serious, it was a mass political school. The problem of ending up being a besieged fortress, that is: we have achieved this, we managed to stop it, but the project outside continues, it is strengthened and we are besieged. So we have to break the siege, that is, we cannot stay inside, we have to go out and fight this political battle. So the first space in which that becomes possible is with, let's say, what was the rebellion at the ballot box of '88 with Cárdenas as candidate.

"And we, within the CEU movement, proposed that as CEU we not take a position in that sense because there were people who did not want to, who held the idea of not voting, of not participating electorally. And there were kids who had sympathy for other parties, there were even kids who came from the PAN, there were some who came from the PRI, few, but there were. So it didn't sound right to us to corporatize a political decision that has to be very individual. And so it was that we formed, those of us who agreed, the MAS, I don't know if you remember, and that was the way for the CEU as such not to be part of a corporate electoral process, but at the same time to make it possible for those of us who wanted to participate. The truth is that university support was massive, without a doubt, but at the same time it made it possible to keep the unity of the movement itself untouched, which was very important.

"And what there was afterward was a resolution as CEU, and it was unanimous, it was to declare Salinas de Gortari a usurper. And that yes, and again we said: 'And we are willing to mobilize again, but not alone because otherwise they are going to beat up only us.' And so there we did propose, in that case, at that time it was to the Engineer: 'We are willing to go as far as a general strike and not only at UNAM, but surely in many more universities, but who else mobilizes? Because if it's just us alone, they are just going to come after us alone.' And that was where we saw that, well, the horse was running out of air, right?, that it wasn't going much further, and we said: 'No, well, we are not going to go around playing the foolish hero either, and we have to take care of this social force.' So, that on the one hand, and on the other, everyone got involved in participating. I would tell you, I don't know, 80 percent of those of us who were ceuístas ended up somewhere, in some way.

"It was a political school because it is the school of struggle that educates, and I have run into them all over the country, everywhere: 'I was from the CEU at CCH Azcapotzalco,' 'I was from the CEU at Naucalpan,' 'I was from Oriente,' 'No, I was already in Political Science'... And there they are doing their work, and so that politicization added to something that, as you rightly said in the introduction, was already coming after the earthquake, from all this social discontent. And so we were no longer the tens of thousands from UNAM, but we started to be millions with this idea that this thing has to change.

"And I believe that as a generation we were that, a generation that fought, that has fought, and the most beautiful thing, that continues fighting. That generation is still there, there it is, and wherever it is, no matter the way and the possibility they have of doing something, they do it.

"And in that sense I believe that as a generation, super proud. Well just imagine, as you say, if the President came out of that movement, how could that movement not be proud? Besides, when the President not only did not betray, but has turned into public policies what were the arguments and demands of that student movement, benefiting millions of young people."

Fabrizio Mejía Madrid, representative of the Faculty of Philosophy and Letters, evokes Sheinbaum in her intervention in front of the porros who had occupied the Che Guevara auditorium on January 16, 1986. Then as now, she has been a woman very committed to her ideas and to Mexico.

"That is a day of the public dialogue, we would arrive early to the public dialogue and occupy the Che Guevara, the auditorium, and it was about seeing how the UNAM bureaucracy made a fool of itself, where Rector Carpizo was not present because his majesty could not come down to speak with the students, but his representatives were there, let's say: José Narro, (José) Sarukhán, all of them were later rectors, (Francisco) Barnés, anyway, and they were there and we raised signs that said CEU and booed the Rectorate's speakers, etcetera.

"And one day the Rectorate says 'enough of this' and at six in the morning porros occupy it. So when we arrived it was full of porros, but I'm telling you people who were bald and had middle-aged potbellies, with sticks inside the Che Guevara, and that image of Claudia Sheinbaum, directing people like 'you, like this, don't fall for provocations,' it's because we were going to come to blows, to shoving at least, and there was going to be a tragedy. So she organizes the exit of the porros, that they leave the first rows, we occupy half and half of the auditorium I think it is, that image is from that day."

Mejía Madrid adds: "You could find Claudia organizing, energizing the rallies, I remember one in which they gave me the microphone on the esplanade and I had never… I said: 'and what do I do now?, if I'm not going to speak, what do I do while they arrive?' And Claudia tells me: 'well, go announcing the contingents.' So I pick up the microphone and all I did was 'CCH Naucalpan is arriving, such and such is arriving,' that was all I said.

"And so I remember those things a lot, but I never imagined she would be President. I saw her as a scientist, as a very diligent, methodical student, and now that I see her and see how she performs, it seems to me I was very wrong: She is a good President."

Ulises Lara, a ceuísta from Political Science, evokes Sheinbaum as a young activist, in mobilizations, for example, against the Halconazo of June 10, 1971, Luis Echeverría's massacre of young demonstrators:

"I had the opportunity to know today's President Claudia Sheinbaum since we were activists. I remember having met her precisely in the preparation of a June 10 march, one more commemoration of the Halconazo, and I want to tell you that, moreover, we could all imagine ourselves in this process of change, but we could hardly say that we were so clear that we were going to win. Shortly after, you will remember, came the democratizing process with the triumph of Cárdenas, as many of us see it, in the Presidency of the Republic. It was a great movement because from the following year, practically as the strike was ending, the Engineer Cárdenas had already left with the Democratic Current of the PRI, a great movement was beginning to take shape. Some of us, at least in my case, had attempted this great process of forming the Mexican Socialist Party. We had Heberto as candidate and we began to convince ourselves that there was a possibility of transforming things through the electoral path. I believe that something we cherished for a long time was that possibility, but that we would have lived it, perhaps we were not so aware of it.

"Andrés's first triumph was very important, the triumph in the city with Cuauhtémoc and then with Andrés Manuel López Obrador himself and the successive ones, but above all the triumph in the country opened up enormous possibilities for us to imagine the nation we wanted and the commitment we had acquired at the university.

"And without a doubt with the arrival of someone like Dr. Claudia Sheinbaum, whom we knew, whom we saw, a completely new expectation opened up because she is a scientist, because she is a compañera in struggle, because she has a vision of the world that we all want to transform, even from an environmentalist perspective, of rescuing what is planetary in the sense of the place where we can all inhabit it.

"And it gives me enormous pleasure to think of being among the fortunate people who were able to know each other not only because there is some kind of link and benefit, as was thought, but because of the possibility of consecrated dreams. We shared many dreams, I mean ideals, I mean proposals and projects that today I believe can be seen realized to a great extent with the arrival of figures, of personalities like Claudia's."

Agustín Guerrero, who was her compañero at CCH Sur, never imagined Sheinbaum in the Presidency of Mexico:

"No, no, to be honest, not only did we never imagine it in the student movement. Never, at that time we were not expecting electoral participation. Even when the CEU was born, the strongest ideological current was opposed to the electoral process. All these lefts that we found in the CEU had a conviction that elections in Mexico were a farce, and we still had a lot of the idea that the path of transformation in the country was through arms. We are the last generation of the legacy of the 1970s when the guerrillas emerged in Mexico, which still has a lot of that thinking, let's say. And there was no vocation for transformation through the ballot box. It is perhaps the figure of Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas that brings that about."

Jesús Ramírez Cuevas, a ceuísta from Political Science, also did not imagine back then the triumph of Sheinbaum as President of Mexico, for whom he is chief of advisers:

"No, we didn't even imagine that the left could come to govern. So I remember a lot that because of everything that the political activism of the CEU generated and the political consequences afterward, and then the defeat the following year, in 1988, of Cuauhtémoc Cárdenas, in the faculties that were dominated by PRI members, like Law and like Political Science, although there were many leftist people, they always sent word to you: 'Why are you in those movements, why are you in the opposition? Better come to the PRI. Here you will be able to change things. Here you will be able to have a space to transform it from within the State.' Because they say that being in the leftist opposition is testimonial, I mean, you are not going to get out of testimony, of martyrdom and of eternal opposition and of the culture of defeat, right? So I believe that fortunately that siren song was never the majority."
