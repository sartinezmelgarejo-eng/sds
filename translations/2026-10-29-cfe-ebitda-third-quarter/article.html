This article by Darren García originally appeared in the October 29, 2026 edition of Contralínea, a Mexican investigative journalism magazine.

The Federal Electricity Commission (CFE) recorded an EBITDA of 164.128 billion pesos, equivalent to a margin of 31.7 percent, from January to September 2026, the state-owned company reported.

This result represented the sixth consecutive quarter with an EBITDA margin above 30 percentage points, the electricity company indicated, adding that its operating income was 90.511 billion pesos, with a margin of 17.5 percent.

In presenting its results, the CFE indicated that its energy sales were 3.4 percent higher and its spending on the main energy sources and fuels was 6.3 percent lower. In the company's words, this shows that it served a greater volume of energy with operational efficiency.

Accumulated net income was 24.313 billion pesos. The CFE explained that the difference compared with the same period of 2025 is mainly due to lower foreign exchange gains from the valuation of obligations in foreign currency, an accounting effect that, it said, does not represent an equivalent cash outflow.

As of September 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents totaled 190.229 billion pesos, 30 percent more than at the close of 2025, while current assets grew 21.6 percent and reached 315.191 billion pesos.

On the other hand, total costs increased 1.3 percent, mainly due to the actuarial update of labor obligations and higher staff compensation and benefits; however, the CFE noted that the savings in energy sources and fuels offset that effect.

On the financial side, interest expenses fell 6.8 percent compared with the same period of 2025, and other net financial expenses were reduced by 27.5 percent. It also reported reductions of 1.2 percent in maintenance, materials, and general services, and of 15.9 percent in taxes and duties.

In addition, short-term debt decreased 4.3 percent, going from 82.809 billion pesos at the close of 2025 to 79.288 billion in September 2026, that is, 3.521 billion pesos less.

For its part, financial leverage fell from 58.6 percent in September 2025 to 52.2 percent in September 2026, a decrease of 6.4 percentage points, which, according to the state-owned company, reflects fewer liabilities relative to its total assets.

In its report, it noted that its capacity to pay its obligations is reflected in its operating generation, the drop in financial expenses, and the increase in its liquidity, which allows it to sustain the investment planned in its 2026-2030 Development Program and to guarantee the supply of electricity.
