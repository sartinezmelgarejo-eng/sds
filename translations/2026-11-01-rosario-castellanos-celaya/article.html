This article by Arturo Sánchez Jiménez originally appeared in the October 31, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Celaya, Guanajuato. The federal government will begin construction next year in this municipality of a campus of the Rosario Castellanos National University, President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo announced this Saturday at a Well-Being Scholarships ceremony at the Industrial and Services Technological High School (CBTIS) 198, where she also asked young people to moderate their use of social media and their exposure to screens.

In front of hundreds of high school students and their families, the president recalled that the institution was born in Mexico City when she was head of government and that it now extends across the whole country. She asserted that her administration will gradually expand scholarships for students at public universities.

Accompanied by Governor Libia Dennise García Muñoz Ledo, Secretary of Education Mario Delgado and other members of her cabinet, the head of the Executive asked the mothers and fathers whether they paid any fee at the school. When they answered yes, she instructed:

She specified that the government will cover the resources that were obtained through that fee. No high school, she said, should charge enrollment or tuition, because education "is a right for all Mexicans."

She reported that in Guanajuato 502,161 primary school children received the school supplies and uniforms scholarship; 264,626 secondary school students, the Rita Cetina scholarship, and 189,502 high school students, the Benito Juárez scholarship, with an investment of 6.338 billion pesos.

Katerin Macías Rodríguez, a scholarship recipient, gave thanks for the support on behalf of her classmates and said that it motivates them to continue their studies, get a job and help their families.

Closing her speech, Sheinbaum urged young people to leave "the screen for a little while." She warned that spending many hours on social media "becomes an addiction" and isolates people, so she invited them to play with their classmates, read a book, exercise, paint or sing.
