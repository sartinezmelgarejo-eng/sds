Mexico City, November 2 (SinEmbargo).— The Ministry of Foreign Affairs (SRE), through the Embassy of Mexico in Saudi Arabia, recommended this Monday avoiding non-essential travel to that country, including tourism and family visits, given the rise in tensions in the Middle East and the level of risk prevailing in the region.

Through a notice posted on social media, the Foreign Ministry urged Mexicans who live in that country or are in transit there to remain alert, take extreme precautions and follow the security instructions of local authorities.

Among the recommendations issued by the diplomatic mission is to pay attention to phone alerts warning of possible attacks, and in the event of receiving such a notification, it asked Mexican nationals to take shelter in a safe place, whether at home or at work.

The Embassy also asked people not to share, under any circumstances, photographs or videos of incidents linked to the hostilities, because this conduct is a crime severely punished in Saudi Arabia.

In addition, it recommended following the Embassy’s social media accounts to receive official and up-to-date information, as well as registering their details in the Mexican mission’s directory, in order to facilitate communication in case of an emergency.

To deal with urgent situations, it made available to Mexican nationals the phone number +966 55 753 9374. Meanwhile, for inquiries and appointments related to passport renewals, it set up the email address cons.arabia_saudita@sre.gob.mx.

Violence in Yemen Worsens the Displacement of the Civilian Population

The Mexican mission’s warning comes amid new hostilities in the Middle East, where the resumption of fighting in Yemen has caused the displacement of hundreds of thousands of people.

According to information from the United Nations (UN), more than 300,000 people have been displaced due to the intensification of fighting in the west of the country. Most of these displacements occurred during the last two months.

The Yemeni civil war began in 2014 and pits the Iran-backed Houthi rebels against government forces supported by Saudi Arabia. After four years of a fragile truce, fighting resumed in July, amid regional tensions between the United States and Iran.

Hostilities intensified in September, when the Houthis took strategic territory on the western coast of the Red Sea. In response, Saudi Arabia increased the bombing by its military coalition in support of the internationally recognized Yemeni government.

Faced with the rise in violence, the International Organization for Migration (IOM) called on the parties involved to protect the civilian population and humanitarian workers, as well as to guarantee safe and unhindered access for humanitarian assistance.

According to DW, Abdusattor Esoev, head of the IOM mission in Yemen, warned that families are once again being forced to leave their homes and that many of them flee with only the clothes they are wearing.

The official said displaced people urgently need shelter, food, drinking water and medical care. He also warned that, without additional funding, thousands of families could be left without the assistance they need.

Meanwhile, as part of the fighting, the Houthi Ministry of Health reported Monday that seven civilians were injured during airstrikes by the Saudi-led coalition on the port of Hodeidah and an oil company in Yemen.
