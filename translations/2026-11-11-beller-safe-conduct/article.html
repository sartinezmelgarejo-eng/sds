Mexico City, November 11 (SinEmbargo).— The authorities of Bolivia authorized this Wednesday the safe conduct so that activist and lawyer Nadia Beller, ex-partner of Argentine consultant Fernando Cerimedo, can leave the country and travel to Mexico, after the Mexican Government granted her diplomatic asylum in view of the risk she reported to her safety.

Through a statement, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Bolivia reported that, after concluding the evaluation of the request submitted on November 4 by the Embassy of Mexico, the competent authorities decided to grant the document. The decision was officially communicated to the Mexican mission yesterday; however, the safe conduct was delivered early this morning.

"The Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in coordination with the Ministry of Government and the General Directorate of Migration, accompanied the corresponding procedure, with Mrs. Beller Delgadillo's departure from the national territory having taken place during the morning of this Wednesday," reads the document.

The Bolivian nation stated that the procedure was carried out in accordance with its legal system and its international obligations, in addition to reaffirming its willingness to maintain relations of dialogue, cooperation and mutual respect with Mexico.

Beller Reported Police Harassment

Before entering the Mexican Embassy in La Paz, Beller reported that the police officers assigned to guard her did not offer her guarantees of safety and, on the contrary, harassed her and restricted her freedom of expression.

In an interview with the Bolivian outlet DTV, she explained that she decided to seek protection on her own after requesting that the officers withdraw from her home. According to her account, she submitted that request on October 29, but the Prosecutor's Office resolution to withdraw the guard was not issued until October 30 and was carried out on November 3.

Beller was the victim of an armed attack on August 17, 2026, when two men disguised as delivery workers shot her outside the Hotel Swiss Contact, in Santa Cruz de la Sierra. The activist was hit by three bullets and survived after being taken to a hospital.

One day after the attack, the Bolivian Police arrested Fernando Cerimedo at the Viru Viru international airport, when he was trying to board a flight bound for Buenos Aires. The courts imposed 180 days of pretrial detention on him, after he was charged as the alleged mastermind of the attack, investigated as attempted femicide.

After being discharged from the hospital, Beller reported that the aggression was aimed at silencing her and requested to enter the Victims and Witnesses Protection Program. She also pointed out alleged irregularities during her hospitalization.

The activist has provided information to the Argentine Federal Prosecutor's Office about an alleged network of bribes and diversion of funds in the National Disability Agency (ANDIS), during the Milei administration. Her statements have also addressed the alleged use of troll farms, disinformation campaigns and the financing of influencers in various countries of Latin America.

In Bolivia, her complaints gave rise to investigations and raids in La Paz, where large amounts of cash, official vehicles and satellite phones were seized, as part of an inquiry that also involves officials of the government of President Rodrigo Paz.
