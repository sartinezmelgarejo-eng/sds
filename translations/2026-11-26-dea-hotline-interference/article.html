This article by Alonso Urrutia and Alma E. Muñoz originally appeared in the November 26, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. President Claudia Sheinbaum condemned the intention of the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) to open a telephone line in that country so that Mexicans can report cases of corruption and links to organized crime in Mexico. She considered this an open strategy of interventionism in Mexico, since there are authorities here who work on security matters.

During her press conference, she also referred to the situation of drug trafficker Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, El Chapo Isidro. Regarding the decision on extraditing him to the United States, in response to that government's request, the president considered that both the Attorney General's Office and the Security Cabinet must weigh what is best for Mexico. It is a decision in which the best alternative to guarantee peace and security in the country must be considered.

However, she rejected the idea that processing this extradition request could be conditioned on other cases, since it must be addressed case by case, based on what is best for Mexico. For that reason, she ruled out that the U.S. decision to deport Inés Gómez Mont, accused of money laundering in Mexico, to Spain would influence the decision.

Sheinbaum said that what corresponds now in the Gómez Mont case is to request her extradition from the government of Spain.

Elaborating on the DEA initiative, Sheinbaum asked how the U.S. government would take it if Mexico's security authorities opened a telephone line for reports of corruption in the United States. She stated that the DEA has a lot to do to fight the drug cartels in the United States before undertaking this initiative related to Mexico.

She referred to information recently released by an international news agency, in which DEA agents allowed the distribution of thousands of fentanyl pills without doing anything to stop it. This is, she said, one of the many cases in which it should act because, she insisted, where does the money from the drugs distributed in that country end up, and where are those arrested for distributing the drugs?
