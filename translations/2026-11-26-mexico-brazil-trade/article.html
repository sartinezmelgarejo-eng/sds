This article by Alma E. Muñoz and Alonso Urrutia originally appeared in the November 26, 2026 edition of La Jornada, Mexico's premier left wing daily newspaper.

Mexico City. After recalling that Petrobras and Petróleos Mexicanos have already signed a Memorandum of Understanding to cooperate in hydrocarbon exploration, production and industrial processes, President Claudia Sheinbaum Pardo stated that with Brazil "we have talked a lot about the complementarity of our economies, more than a free trade agreement," because that would affect both countries "on some issues."

We continue working on that and also, she added, on cultural and scientific activities and a very close collaboration.

"The only thing left, or what they are discussing at this moment, is under what conditions the joint coordination would be carried out. Brazil uses licensing; in Mexico, production licenses are not in the Constitution, so what we are seeking from our perspective is a mixed investment. We are talking about this."

Weighing Attending Lula's Inauguration

She mentioned that dates are still pending for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva to come to Mexico and for her to go to Brazil. She also said she is considering attending the president's inauguration. "We'll see if we can go, depending on the situation in the country. It is January 5," she indicated.

When told that in that country there is a lot of curiosity about Mexican humanism, Sheinbaum Pardo emphasized that the vision is the well-being of the Mexican people "and rather than copying models from other places in the world, it arises from our own history."

She highlighted that poverty has been reduced for more than 17 million Mexicans, as recognized by the World Bank, and added that labor poverty fell from 34 to 30.5 percent and the annual percentage change in real per capita labor income nationwide stands at 9.3 percent.

She anticipated that on December 1, when eight years of the transformation will be completed, she will present the achievements reached. "People are content, happy, happy, happy, as President (Andrés Manuel) López Obrador would say."
